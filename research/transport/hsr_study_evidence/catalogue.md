# East Coast High-Speed Rail Study Evidence Catalogue

**Purpose:** reuse historical and current evidence about east-coast geography, demand, engineering and city systems without inheriting any proposal as VECA's preferred route.

## Study lineage

### 1991 — Very Fast Train (VFT)

A major private-sector proposal for a Sydney–Canberra–Melbourne very-fast train, with possible extension toward Brisbane.

Later Commonwealth HSR work explicitly treats the VFT demand work as part of the historical evidence base.

**VECA value:** early intercapital demand assumptions, regional-development expectations, technology/cost assumptions, and evidence about why private financing alone failed.

**Status:** historical proposal / evidence source, not an endorsed route.

### 1999 — Speedrail

The Speedrail consortium developed a Sydney–Canberra high-speed proposal during the 1990s tender process. The process ended in 2000 when the proposal did not satisfy the Commonwealth's no-net-cost-to-government criterion.

The 2013 HSR demand appendix explicitly reviews Speedrail alongside the VFT and later studies.

**VECA value:** Sydney–Canberra demand, station and access assumptions, financing structure, government-risk allocation, and comparison with later demand models.

**Status:** historical proposal / evidence source.

### 2001 — East Coast Very High Speed Train Scoping Study

Commonwealth-commissioned `East Coast Very High Speed Train Scoping Study — Stage 1 Preliminary Study Final Report`, November 2001.

The report considered an east-coast very-high-speed system at continental scale and concluded that such a project would be of national economic and regional significance, while also recognising the scale of public-sector leadership and funding likely to be required.

**VECA value:**
- early full east-coast corridor screening;
- regional-development framing;
- intermodal competition;
- demand and funding assumptions;
- engineering and route constraints;
- a 2001 benchmark for how east-coast settlement and travel expectations have changed.

### 2010–2011 — Commonwealth HSR Study Phase 1

The Commonwealth announced a $20 million two-phase HSR feasibility study in 2010. Phase 1, released in 2011, shortlisted corridors and station options and produced preliminary demand and cost estimates for an east-coast network.

**VECA value:**
- formal corridor shortlisting;
- comparative route/station options;
- preliminary capital-cost structure;
- demand assumptions and travel-market segmentation.

### 2013 — Commonwealth HSR Study Phase 2

The Phase 2 study greatly expanded the engineering, route, demand, cost, environmental and economic analysis.

Headline characteristics of the preferred study alignment included approximately 1,748 km of dedicated HSR between Brisbane, Sydney, Canberra and Melbourne, with a coastal alignment north of Sydney and inland alignment south of Sydney plus spur lines to Gold Coast and Canberra.

The study analysed station options for regional centres including Casino, Grafton, Coffs Harbour, Port Macquarie, Taree, Newcastle, Central Coast, Southern Highlands, Wagga Wagga, Albury-Wodonga and Shepparton.

The full preferred alignment was estimated at about $114 billion in 2012 dollars. The work also identified substantial tunnelling requirements, particularly through Sydney approaches.

**VECA value:** this is the deepest historical technical corpus currently identified and should be mined appendix-by-appendix rather than treated merely as a single report.

Priority evidence families:
- travel markets and demand;
- corridor/alignment screening;
- station-location assessment;
- capital-cost estimating;
- tunnelling / structures / geotechnical constraints;
- environmental constraints;
- economic appraisal;
- staging;
- regional accessibility;
- aviation substitution;
- city-shaping assumptions.

### 2017–2018 — Parliamentary transport-connectivity / settlement-rebalancing work

Parliamentary inquiry material revisited HSR not only as transport but as a possible settlement-shaping mechanism. Evidence included proposals explicitly arguing for new regional cities and population redistribution along an east-coast HSR spine.

**VECA value:** this material is especially relevant because it separates two distinct philosophies:
1. HSR primarily as intercapital transport; and
2. HSR as an instrument of national spatial restructuring.

VECA must not assume either philosophy is correct, but should preserve the distinction.

### 2023 onward — High Speed Rail Authority

The High Speed Rail Authority was established to progress an east-coast network, initially focusing on Sydney–Newcastle.

Its corporate plan states that it is building on the 2013 Phase 2 work, reviewing the earlier study and preparing an east-coast Pathway to Delivery plus a Sydney–Newcastle business case.

By 2025 the Sydney–Newcastle business case had been provided to government and Infrastructure Australia had evaluated it. Publicly released departmental material indicates that the business case recommends further work to refine design, costs and schedule before any construction decision.

**VECA value:**
- the latest re-test of the Sydney–Newcastle corridor;
- updated construction-cost environment;
- current route/station/city-shaping thinking;
- current environmental and land-use constraints;
- a direct comparison with the 2013 assumptions.

## Cross-study questions for VECA

For every substantial study, extract not just its recommendation but its underlying evidence.

### What stayed the same?
Repeated findings across decades are potentially high-value evidence:
- difficult terrain crossings;
- tunnel-heavy metropolitan approaches;
- recurring regional station candidates;
- strong/weak travel markets;
- corridor-protection pressure;
- airport-substitution opportunities;
- infrastructure bottlenecks.

### What changed?
Track changes in:
- construction cost;
- population forecasts;
- air travel volumes;
- commuting patterns;
- regional-centre growth;
- urban footprint;
- environmental constraints;
- technology;
- financing assumptions;
- value placed on regional development.

### What was assumed rather than demonstrated?
Pay particular attention to:
- CBD-to-CBD station assumptions;
- requirement for sub-three-hour capital-city travel;
- regional stations treated as secondary to intercapital demand;
- assumptions that regional development follows automatically from HSR;
- population forecasts that are now directly testable against 2025 ABS data.

## Doctrine guardrail

The presence of a corridor in multiple HSR studies does **not** make it VECA's preferred corridor.

Repeated study attention can indicate that the corridor has been repeatedly investigated, not that it is optimal under VECA's settlement-first doctrine.
