# East Coast High-Speed Rail Study Evidence Catalogue

**Purpose:** reuse historical and current evidence about east-coast geography, demand, engineering and city systems without inheriting any proposal as VECA's preferred route.

## Study lineage

### 1991 — Very Fast Train (VFT)

A major private-sector proposal for a Sydney–Canberra–Melbourne very-fast train, with possible extension toward Brisbane. Later Commonwealth HSR work explicitly treats the VFT demand work as part of the historical evidence base.

**VECA value:** early intercapital demand assumptions, regional-development expectations, technology/cost assumptions, and evidence about why private financing alone failed.

**Status:** historical proposal / evidence source, not an endorsed route.

### 1999 — Speedrail

The Speedrail consortium developed a Sydney–Canberra high-speed proposal during the 1990s tender process. The process ended in 2000 when the proposal did not satisfy the Commonwealth's no-net-cost-to-government criterion. The 2013 HSR demand appendix explicitly reviews Speedrail alongside the VFT and later studies.

**VECA value:** Sydney–Canberra demand, station and access assumptions, financing structure, government-risk allocation, and comparison with later demand models.

### 2001 — East Coast Very High Speed Train Scoping Study

Commonwealth-commissioned `East Coast Very High Speed Train Scoping Study — Stage 1 Preliminary Study Final Report`, November 2001.

The report considered an east-coast very-high-speed system at continental scale: technologies, corridors, operating performance, travel time, capital/operating cost, demand, financing, national-interest economics and regional development.

Contemporary/secondary references report the study itself at approximately $2.3 million; retain that figure as **secondary until independently confirmed from a primary expenditure record**.

**VECA value:** early full east-coast corridor screening; regional-development framing; intermodal competition; demand/funding assumptions; engineering constraints; and a 2001 benchmark whose forecasts can now be tested against observed 2025 population and aviation data.

### 2010–2013 — Commonwealth HSR Study Phases 1 and 2

The Commonwealth announced a **$20 million two-phase feasibility study** in 2010. Phase 1 (2011) shortlisted corridors/stations and preliminary demand/costs. Phase 2 (2013) substantially deepened engineering, alignment, demand, economic, environmental and staging analysis.

Headline Phase 2 preferred-study system: about 1,748 km of dedicated HSR and $114 billion in 2012 dollars.

The study considered regional centres including Casino, Grafton, Coffs Harbour, Port Macquarie, Taree, Newcastle, Central Coast, Southern Highlands, Wagga Wagga, Albury-Wodonga and Shepparton.

**VECA value:** deepest historical technical corpus currently identified. Mine appendix-by-appendix rather than treating it as one recommendation.

Priority evidence families:
- travel markets and demand;
- corridor/alignment screening;
- station-location assessment;
- capital-cost estimating and quantities;
- tunnelling / structures / geotechnical constraints;
- environmental constraints;
- economic appraisal and value capture;
- staging;
- regional accessibility;
- aviation substitution;
- city-shaping assumptions;
- land and corridor preservation.

### 2015–2016 — Infrastructure Australia regional rail constraint evaluation

Infrastructure Australia recorded Newcastle–Sydney and Wollongong–Sydney rail connectivity as a national infrastructure problem. It identified slow services, winding legacy alignments, the Hawkesbury River and Illawarra Escarpment as major physical constraints and considered a hierarchy of interventions from fleet/operations through deviations, grade improvements and new major crossings.

**VECA value:** a useful bridge between HSR and incremental rail. It allows the project to compare the cost/benefit ceiling of upgrading inherited nineteenth-century alignments against building new corridors.

### 2017–2018 — Parliamentary transport-connectivity / settlement-rebalancing work

Parliamentary inquiry material revisited HSR not only as transport but as a possible settlement-shaping mechanism. Evidence included proposals explicitly arguing for new regional cities and population redistribution along an east-coast HSR spine.

**VECA value:** separates two philosophies that must not be conflated: HSR as intercapital transport versus HSR as an instrument of national spatial restructuring.

### 2019 — Sydney–Newcastle Fast Rail Strategic Business Case

Australian and NSW governments completed a Sydney–Newcastle strategic business case in 2019. Infrastructure Australia states it tested a range from incremental upgrades to transformational investments. The current HSRA business case explicitly builds on this work as well as the 2013 HSR study.

The public strategic business case itself has not been identified as a fully released primary document; later government/Infrastructure Australia material confirms its existence and role.

**VECA value:** crucial intermediate evidence generation. It tested whether a legacy-line upgrade/faster-rail strategy could address the same corridor before the current dedicated-HSR program.

### 2020–2023 — Faster Rail planning / Major Project Business Case work

The Australian Government subsequently committed **$15 million** for Sydney–Newcastle Faster Rail planning under the Major Project Business Case program. Its stated purpose was to finalise scope, cost, timing and delivery of priority projects and support detailed Infrastructure Australia assessment.

Other east-coast faster-rail business cases were also funded or developed, including Sydney–Wollongong, Sydney–Parkes, Melbourne–Albury, Melbourne–Traralgon, Brisbane–Gold Coast and Toowoomba–Brisbane passenger rail work. These are not all HSR proposals, but their corridor engineering, demand and regional-development evidence may be reusable by VECA.

**VECA value:** this broadens the evidence library beyond the single Brisbane–Sydney–Melbourne HSR spine and gives comparative evidence for inland and regional connectivity.

### 2023 onward — High Speed Rail Authority / Newcastle–Sydney Line 1

The High Speed Rail Authority was established to progress an east-coast network, initially focusing on Sydney–Newcastle. The 2025 Infrastructure Australia Stage 3 evaluation says the current business case builds on the 2013 and 2019 work and tested alternative modes, station locations and alignments.

Infrastructure Australia supported the development phase but recommended substantially greater design maturity, cost certainty, network-integration analysis, housing analysis and funding work before final investment. The total construction capital cost remains publicly withheld for commercial sensitivity.

In 2026 the Commonwealth commenced a **$659.6 million, two-year development phase** intended to make Line 1 construction-ready. Current procurement includes major tunnelling and underground-station design/construction planning.

**VECA value:** modern Australian benchmark for design, approvals, tunnelling, station construction, procurement, labour/productivity, cost escalation, housing integration and corridor protection. The development process may be more useful to VECA than the final preferred route.

## Study expenditure itself is evidence

VECA should separately track how much Australia spends learning about infrastructure before construction.

Confirmed examples now include:
- 2010–2013 east-coast HSR feasibility study: **$20m**;
- later Sydney–Newcastle Faster Rail planning/business-case program: **$15m** Commonwealth program cost;
- current Newcastle–Sydney HSR development phase: **$659.6m** over two years.

These are different scopes and price years and must not be compared as like-for-like 'study costs'. The current development phase includes design maturity, approvals, market/contractor work and construction-readiness activity well beyond a desktop feasibility study.

This evidence family is nevertheless important to VECA's separate infrastructure-cost research: **pre-construction knowledge, approvals and risk retirement can itself become a major infrastructure expenditure class.**

## Cross-study questions for VECA

For every substantial study, extract not just its recommendation but its underlying evidence.

### What stayed the same?
Repeated findings across decades are potentially high-value evidence: difficult terrain crossings; tunnel-heavy metropolitan approaches; recurring regional station/catchment questions; strong/weak travel markets; corridor-protection pressure; airport substitution; and infrastructure bottlenecks.

### What changed?
Track construction cost, population forecasts, air travel, commuting, regional-centre growth, urban footprint, environmental constraints, technology, financing assumptions and value placed on regional development.

### What was assumed rather than demonstrated?
Pay particular attention to CBD-to-CBD assumptions, sub-three-hour intercapital objectives, regional stations treated as secondary, claims that regional development follows automatically, and population forecasts now directly testable against the 2025 ABS dataset.

## Evidence extraction schema

Each study should eventually produce structured records with:
`study_id`, `study_year`, `study_title`, `sponsor`, `study_type`, `corridor_segment`, `evidence_theme`, `finding`, `quantitative_value`, `unit`, `price_year`, `source_page`, `source_url`, `confidence`, `repeated_in_other_studies`, `vega_relevance`, `proposal_specific_assumption`.

## Doctrine guardrail

The presence of a corridor in multiple HSR studies does **not** make it VECA's preferred corridor. Repeated study attention can indicate that a corridor has been repeatedly investigated, not that it is optimal under VECA's settlement-first doctrine.
