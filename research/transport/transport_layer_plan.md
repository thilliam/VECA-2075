# EXP-001 Transport Layer Plan

## Goal

Build a coherent evidence layer for the inherited east-coast transport system before proposing any future transport network.

Transport must be represented as a **system**, not as a collection of lines on a map. The layer should capture where infrastructure exists, how it is used, where major bottlenecks occur, what capital is already committed, and what decades of prior corridor studies have learned about the physical and economic geography.

## Layer families

### 1. Existing road network

Capture nationally consistent foundation geometry first, then enrich major corridors with:
- motorway / highway classification;
- freight significance;
- traffic counts where available;
- heavy-vehicle share where available;
- congestion / reliability indicators where available;
- current major upgrade projects.

Primary initial sources:
- Geoscience Australia / national foundation spatial data;
- National Freight Data Hub harmonised traffic counts;
- state road agencies for authoritative local detail.

### 2. Existing rail network

Capture:
- passenger and freight rail geometry;
- interstate standard-gauge corridors;
- regional passenger routes;
- major freight-only corridors;
- intermodal terminals;
- ports and major industrial rail interfaces;
- current freight-use intensity where supported by data.

The National Freight Data Hub freight-train products provide train counts, gross mass and speed on mapped rail segments using locomotive GPS plus ARTC schedule/attribute data. Their coverage is strongest on the interstate / ARTC network and must not be treated as complete coverage of all intrastate freight rail.

### 3. Airports

Capture at minimum:
- Brisbane;
- Gold Coast;
- Newcastle;
- Western Sydney International;
- Sydney Kingsford Smith;
- Canberra;
- Melbourne;
- Avalon;
- significant regional airports where passenger/freight scale or future optionality is material.

For each airport capture:
- passenger volume;
- freight role where material;
- runway / curfew / capacity constraints where authoritative evidence exists;
- recent/committed capital works;
- relationship to surrounding employment and settlement.

### 4. Ports and intermodal logistics

Capture:
- Port of Brisbane;
- Port of Newcastle;
- Port Botany;
- Port Kembla;
- Port of Melbourne;
- other material east-coast ports;
- Moorebank and other major intermodal terminals;
- Inland Rail interfaces;
- freight catchments where available.

### 5. Recent and committed transport capital

Overlay:
- completed roughly 2015–2026;
- under construction;
- funded / committed;
- approved not started;
- planned high confidence;
- proposed.

Do not merge these categories.

### 6. Historical and current HSR evidence library

Treat historical east-coast high-speed-rail studies as an evidence corpus, not as route recommendations.

The corpus should extract reusable evidence on:
- corridor geometry;
- terrain and tunnelling;
- bridge / viaduct requirements;
- construction risks;
- station catchments;
- travel markets;
- airport substitution;
- regional accessibility;
- demand forecasts and sensitivities;
- city-shaping assumptions;
- environmental constraints;
- indicative capital costs and their price-year basis;
- corridor-protection issues;
- staging logic.

Do **not** inherit their preferred route, station locations, CBD-centric assumptions, population forecasts or technology choices without re-testing them against VECA doctrine.

## Key discipline

VECA is not designing HSR in EXP-001.

Historical HSR material is used because Australia has repeatedly paid for detailed investigation of the same east-coast geography. Repeated findings across independent study generations are especially valuable evidence.

## Completion criteria

The transport layer is complete enough for EXP-001 when:

1. major road, rail, airport, port and intermodal assets are represented;
2. nationally consistent base geometry is established or an explicit gap is documented;
3. major freight-use evidence is represented with source limitations;
4. major completed/committed transport capital is status-tagged;
5. the principal historical/current HSR studies are catalogued and their reusable evidence themes indexed;
6. no preferred future VECA route or station network has been inferred from this inherited evidence layer.
