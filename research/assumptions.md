# Assumptions and Open Questions

**Updated:** 9 September 2026

This is the project assumption ledger. It separates project-design facts, working assumptions, analytical concepts and unresolved questions. Root `PROJECT_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP.md` contains the current stage plan.

## Known project facts

- VECA's horizon is approximately 2075.
- The base geographic scope is eastern Australia from South East Queensland to Melbourne, including plausible inland regions.
- South Australia is excluded from the base analysis and reserved for a later inclusion test.
- The initial population-growth forcing scenario is approximately 10 million additional residents.
- The 10 million figure is a scenario input, not a VECA population forecast.
- EXP-001 has established a first coherent inherited-system evidence base and a layered-map POC; enrichment continues.
- EXP-002 habitat/resource screening is the current primary analytical stage.
- No VECA high-speed-rail route and no preferred candidate new city has been authorised.
- Government intent/future-capital optionality is an evidence family, not a requirement to follow current government plans.
- Regional Anchor Cluster joining/weighting is currently paused until physical survival screening is more mature.

## Working assumptions — to test

- Concentrating most additional population inside the existing Brisbane, Sydney and Melbourne metropolitan footprints may not be the best 2075 outcome.
- Several deliberately expanded or new regional population centres may produce better system outcomes.
- Recent and committed capital contains useful information about future opportunity, but raw expenditure is not a sufficient measure.
- Some future public capital is locationally flexible because it represents renewal or growth-triggered service capacity that governments will need to fund somewhere regardless.
- Future water supply can be partly treated as an engineering-economic choice rather than a fixed natural endowment, within environmental and resilience limits.
- Renewable generation, storage and transmission geography may materially change the attractiveness of future settlement and industrial locations.
- Long-lived corridor and land reservations may have high option value even when corresponding infrastructure is not justified immediately.
- Universities, tertiary health and specialist institutions can sometimes shape settlement gravity rather than merely follow population.
- Schools are predominantly population-following infrastructure and may be useful as a measurable growth-triggered capital liability.
- AI and automation may weaken some current reasons for tight employment agglomeration, but could also strengthen clustering around compute, capital, universities and technical ecosystems.
- Deliberate decentralisation should preferably be achieved through voluntary household and business choice: create places whose combined housing, connectivity, services, economic opportunity and lifestyle proposition is sufficiently attractive that people choose them.

None of these assumptions should be encoded as a conclusion or scoring advantage without evidence.

## Analytical concepts — documented, not yet composite-weighted

### Capital Gravity
Strategically useful public and private investment accumulating around a location. Must distinguish enabling investment from remediation of congestion or legacy deficiencies.

### Future Capital Optionality
How movable future capital remains. Current conceptual classes include fixed inheritance, committed low-flex capital, planned capital, renewal liability, growth-triggered capital and optionality assets such as reserved land/corridors.

### Redirectable future capital
The portion of future expenditure likely to occur regardless of VECA whose location, service model or sequencing may still be changed. This is central to comparing alternative settlement cost with the default-growth counterfactual.

### Infrastructure inheritance per additional resident
The amount and type of genuinely new enabling infrastructure required to support additional population after usable inherited capacity and unavoidable future renewal are accounted for.

### Habitat suitability
Future climate, hazards, amenity and land characteristics affecting long-term liveability.

### Resource suitability
Water, energy, land and food-system interactions that enable or constrain settlement and industry.

### Economic potential
Capacity to support productive, diverse employment rather than primarily dormitory settlement growth.

### Network value
The effect of locating population or industry in a place on the efficiency, capacity and resilience of the wider national system.

### Anchor Asset Cluster — paused methodology
A possible derived object representing complementary assets within functional travel-time catchments: health, university, VET, land, transport, water, energy and industry. The concept is useful but its join/weighting work is paused until EXP-002 physical screening matures.

If resumed, individual components must remain visible. Potential node dimensions include scale, reach, scarcity, replacement cost, scalability, strategic coupling and lock-in/optionality.

### Human Capital Formation Capacity
How readily a region can educate/train the professional, technical, trade and service workforce needed for substantial settlement/economic growth. Keep university/research, clinical training, VET/trades, schools and workforce attraction distinct.

### AI Infrastructure Potential
Physical suitability for AI-intensive infrastructure/economy: electricity, transmission, fibre, cooling climate, land, water/recycling, universities/research and compatible industrial loads. Not a data-centre siting score yet.

### AI Economic Resilience
Ability of a regional economy to remain productive/diverse across different automation outcomes.

### AI Social Resilience
Whether a place remains highly desirable if conventional paid work occupies materially less time: housing quality, accessibility, nature, recreation, sport, education, health, culture and community infrastructure may become more important.

### Life accessibility
How much of residents' desired daily/weekly life is reachable within practical 15/30/60-minute bands. Do not quantify until definitions and value assumptions are explicit.

### Voluntary migration / revealed attractiveness
Why households and businesses would choose a future settlement over alternatives. Engineering capacity alone is insufficient.

## AI / automation scenario family — future stress tests

- `A0 — limited disruption`: employment geography broadly resembles today.
- `A1 — productivity transformation`: most remain employed but paid working time falls materially.
- `A2 — major occupational displacement`: substantial work disappears or changes profoundly.
- `A3 — weaker location dependence`: knowledge work becomes less tied to major metropolitan employment centres.

Also test the opposite: AI may strengthen agglomeration around universities, compute, capital and elite technical ecosystems.

These are stress tests, not probability-weighted forecasts.

## Open questions — current

### EXP-002 / physical screen
- Which projection products/scenarios provide the best comparable 2050/2070 climate surface across jurisdictions?
- What spatial unit should the survival map use: grid cells, statistical areas, functional regions or hybrid?
- What slope/terrain thresholds are meaningful as cost burdens without becoming arbitrary exclusions?
- How should bushfire, flood and coastal hazards be represented where national datasets are incomplete or old?
- What agricultural/ecological datasets best represent national opportunity cost?
- How should Indigenous rights/cultural values be represented without reducing them to a simplistic numerical constraint?
- How should water augmentation pathways and sustainable yield be compared consistently across regions?

### Candidate/cluster methodology
- What evidence threshold promotes an area from broad survival to detailed candidate investigation?
- When Anchor Cluster work resumes, should catchments use 15/30/60-minute road/public-transport bands, labour-market regions, or multiple views?
- How should scarcity and scalability of hospitals/universities/freight/energy assets be compared without a premature composite score?

### Economics / capital
- How should VECA distinguish enabling capital from remediation, replacement and growth-triggered expenditure?
- How can renewal windows for hospitals, schools, water plants and other public assets be evidenced consistently?
- What is the right counterfactual: current official plans, metropolitan-concentration scenario, or several baselines?
- At what stage should land value, construction cost and housing affordability enter?
- Is whole-system incremental infrastructure cost per additional resident the right main cost denominator?

### Settlement attractiveness
- Can life accessibility be measured without embedding one lifestyle preference?
- What evidence would demonstrate that a regional centre could attract population voluntarily rather than merely accommodate it?
- How should economic diversity and agglomeration benefits be measured at future larger city scales?

### Transport
- How should capacity/bottlenecks be valued alongside distance and speed?
- Where do conventional/faster regional rail and freight improvements dominate HSR/new-corridor options?
- What freight role, if any, is appropriate for future high-speed/light-container systems?

### Scope
- What evidence would justify running `EXP-SA-001` early?
- When should digital/fibre/data-centre geography become a full domain rather than a later overlay?

## Implementation choices

The map POC currently uses MapLibre GL JS, but this is not a permanent architecture decision. Production spatial serving, database, tiling, optimisation and scoring methods remain open until analytical need justifies them.
