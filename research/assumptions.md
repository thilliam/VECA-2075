# Assumptions and Open Questions

This is an initial assumption ledger. It deliberately separates known framing facts, working assumptions and open questions. Items should move categories only when evidence justifies the move.

## Known project facts

These are facts about the VECA research design, not claims about future Australia.

- VECA's horizon is approximately 2075.
- The base geographic scope is eastern Australia from South East Queensland to Melbourne, including plausible inland regions.
- South Australia is excluded from the base analysis and reserved for a later inclusion test.
- The initial population-growth forcing scenario is approximately 10 million additional residents.
- That 10 million figure is a scenario input, not a VECA population forecast.
- EXP-001 will map the inherited spatial system before proposing new settlement or transport architecture.
- No high-speed rail route and no candidate new city will be proposed in EXP-001.

## Working assumptions — to test

- Concentrating most additional population inside the existing Brisbane, Sydney and Melbourne metropolitan footprints may not be the best 2075 outcome.
- Several deliberately expanded or new regional population centres may produce better system outcomes.
- The geography of recent and committed capital investment contains useful information about future opportunity, but raw expenditure is not a sufficient measure.
- Future water supply can be partly treated as an engineering-economic choice rather than a fixed natural endowment, within environmental and resilience limits.
- Renewable generation, storage and transmission geography may materially change the attractiveness of future settlement and industrial locations.
- Long-lived corridor and land reservations may have high option value even when the corresponding infrastructure is not justified immediately.
- AI and automation may weaken some current reasons for very tight employment agglomeration, but could also strengthen clustering around compute, capital, universities and technical ecosystems. VECA should test both directions rather than predict one.
- Deliberate decentralisation should preferably be achieved through voluntary household and business choice: create places whose combined housing, connectivity, services, economic opportunity and lifestyle proposition is sufficiently attractive that people choose them.

None of these assumptions should be encoded as a conclusion or scoring advantage without evidence.

## Future analytical concepts — document only

These concepts may later contribute to a settlement-suitability framework. **No formula, implementation or weighting is authorised at bootstrap.**

### Capital Gravity
The strategically useful public and private investment accumulating around a location. Must distinguish enabling investment from remediation of congestion or legacy deficiencies.

### Infrastructure inheritance per additional resident
The amount and type of new enabling infrastructure required to support additional population after accounting for usable inherited capacity.

### Habitat suitability
Future climate, hazards, amenity and land characteristics affecting long-term liveability.

### Resource suitability
Water, energy, land and food-system interactions that enable or constrain settlement and industry.

### Economic potential
Capacity to support productive, diverse employment rather than primarily dormitory settlement.

### Network value
The effect of locating population or industry in a place on the efficiency and resilience of the wider national system.

### AI Infrastructure Potential
The physical suitability of a location for an AI-intensive economy: electricity availability and cost, transmission capacity, fibre/digital connectivity, climate and cooling conditions, suitable land, water and recycled-water options, proximity to universities/research capability, and opportunities to integrate compute infrastructure with compatible industrial loads. This is not a data-centre siting score yet.

### AI Economic Resilience
The ability of a proposed regional economy to remain productive and diverse across substantially different automation outcomes. Future work may examine occupational and industry concentration, automation exposure, remote-work potential and capacity to generate new forms of economic activity. Avoid creating a settlement whose economic base is overwhelmingly exposed to the same technological transition.

### AI Social Resilience
Whether a place remains an exceptionally good place to live if conventional paid work occupies materially less of people's lives — for example, if a significant share of residents works 25–30 rather than roughly 38 hours per week. This may increase the strategic value of housing quality, local accessibility, natural amenity, recreation, sport, education, health, culture and community infrastructure. The hours are scenario values, not forecasts.

### Life accessibility
A possible complement to conventional economic accessibility: how much of a resident's desired daily and weekly life is reachable within practical 15/30/60-minute travel bands. Candidate components include nature, sport, schools, health, university, culture, social infrastructure, walking/cycling and regional transport. Do not quantify until definitions and evidence are established.

### Voluntary migration / revealed attractiveness
A future settlement should not be judged only by whether engineering can support a target population. VECA should eventually ask why households and businesses would voluntarily choose it over alternatives. This concept should combine housing, employment/economic opportunity, connectivity, services, amenity and lifestyle without assuming that government-directed relocation is the primary mechanism.

These concepts may eventually be combined, but doing so before the evidence base is understood risks false precision and hidden value judgements.

## AI / automation scenario family — future stress tests

AI is a long-horizon uncertainty to design robustness around, not something VECA must forecast correctly. A later experiment may stress-test candidate settlement systems against scenarios such as:

- `A0 — limited disruption`: employment geography and conventional working patterns remain broadly similar to today.
- `A1 — productivity transformation`: most people remain employed but average paid working time falls materially.
- `A2 — major occupational displacement`: a significant share of today's work disappears or changes profoundly.
- `A3 — weaker location dependence`: AI-enabled and knowledge work becomes substantially less tied to major metropolitan employment centres.

These are deliberately broad test conditions, not probability-weighted forecasts. Additional scenarios should include the possibility that AI strengthens rather than weakens agglomeration.

## Open questions

- What population horizon and demographic scenarios should accompany the 10 million forcing case?
- What spatial unit should later comparison use: grid cells, statistical areas, functional regions, corridors or a hybrid?
- How should VECA distinguish genuinely enabling capital from remediation or replacement expenditure?
- What minimum evidence standard should promote a project from `proposed` to `planned_high_confidence`?
- Which future-climate scenarios and time slices provide the most decision-useful 2050–2075 view?
- How should Indigenous cultural values and rights be represented without reducing them to a simplistic numerical constraint layer?
- What agricultural and ecological datasets best capture national opportunity cost rather than merely protected-area boundaries?
- How should water augmentation options be compared consistently across regions?
- At what stage should land value, construction cost and housing affordability enter the model?
- What evidence would justify running `EXP-SA-001` early rather than after the east-coast base model?
- Which AI/automation scenarios are sufficiently distinct and decision-useful without pretending to forecast 2075 labour markets?
- Can life accessibility be measured without embedding an arbitrary lifestyle preference into the model?
- What evidence would demonstrate that a proposed regional centre could attract population voluntarily rather than merely accommodate it physically?

## Deliberately unresolved implementation choices

No decision has yet been made on GIS software, database, programming language, web framework, visualisation stack, map tiling, spatial resolution, optimisation method or scoring model. EXP-001 should choose only the minimum tooling required once its source formats and analytical needs are known.
