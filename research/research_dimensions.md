# Research Dimensions

This file defines the initial research surface. It is a coverage guide, not a scoring model. No weights are assigned at bootstrap.

## Population

Investigate:
- current population and density;
- existing cities and towns;
- demographic trends;
- official long-range population projections; and
- housing capacity where relevant.

Current population describes the inherited system; it must not become an automatic proxy for future settlement suitability.

## Existing transport

Map major:
- passenger rail;
- freight rail;
- motorways;
- highways;
- airports;
- ports; and
- intermodal terminals.

Where useful, distinguish capacity, service level, freight/passenger function and constraints.

## Recent infrastructure investment — approximately 2015–2026

Capture major public and private investment with reliable figures where available. This provides one input to later analysis of capital momentum and inherited infrastructure.

Avoid treating expenditure alone as strategic value: some large investments create new capacity while others remediate congestion or legacy deficiencies.

## Committed infrastructure

Identify projects that are:
- `under_construction`;
- `funded_committed`; or
- `approved_not_started`.

Keep these distinct from announcements and proposals.

## Planned infrastructure

Record credible government and private-sector pipelines that are not yet committed. Capture status and confidence explicitly. The controlled project status `planned_high_confidence` should be used only where evidence supports it; otherwise use `proposed`.

## Electricity

Map and investigate:
- renewable generation;
- Renewable Energy Zones;
- major transmission;
- interconnectors;
- storage;
- major substations; and
- major proposed energy projects.

Later work should consider both energy availability and the network consequences of placing new load in different regions.

## Water

Investigate:
- major dams and storages;
- catchments;
- groundwater;
- desalination;
- recycled-water systems;
- major pipelines; and
- water-security constraints.

Later analysis should consider engineering options, energy requirements, resilience and marginal cost of supporting additional residents and industry.

## Climate and hazards

Use projected 2050–2075 conditions where credible datasets permit. Relevant dimensions include:
- heat;
- humidity;
- drought;
- bushfire;
- flood;
- cyclone;
- sea-level rise; and
- extreme weather.

Record scenario and time horizon with future-climate data.

## Terrain and land

Investigate:
- elevation;
- slope;
- geology where relevant;
- buildable land;
- agricultural value;
- conservation areas;
- Indigenous cultural values where suitable authoritative spatial evidence exists; and
- other material land constraints.

## Economy

Map major:
- industrial centres;
- ports and logistics;
- mining and resources;
- manufacturing;
- universities and research;
- defence;
- health; and
- employment clusters.

Later analysis should distinguish productive employment potential from dormitory settlement growth.

## Digital infrastructure — later dimension

Potential future layers include:
- fibre backbones;
- subsea cable landings;
- data-centre clusters; and
- communications resilience.

This is not required for the first coherent EXP-001 base map unless evidence shows it is necessary.

## AI Infrastructure Potential — future layer

Investigate the physical potential for AI-intensive infrastructure and related industry, eventually considering:
- electricity availability, reliability and cost;
- transmission capacity and augmentation;
- fibre and communications connectivity;
- climate and cooling conditions;
- suitable industrial land;
- water and recycled-water options;
- proximity to universities and research capability; and
- compatible industrial loads or infrastructure synergies.

This layer is conceptual only at this stage. Do not rank regions or assume that attracting data centres is intrinsically beneficial.

## AI Economic Resilience — future layer

Investigate whether a regional economic system remains diverse and productive under materially different automation outcomes. Potential later evidence includes industry and occupational concentration, automation exposure, remote-work potential, education/retraining capability and capacity to create new economic activity.

This is a resilience test, not a forecast of which occupations AI will eliminate.

## AI Social Resilience — future layer

Investigate whether a settlement remains highly desirable if paid employment occupies materially less of residents' time. Potential later dimensions include housing quality and affordability, 15/30/60-minute life accessibility, natural amenity, recreation, sport, education, health, culture, community infrastructure, walking/cycling and regional connectivity.

Natural amenity may become more economically significant if leisure time rises, but VECA must test rather than assume that outcome.

## Capital Gravity — future concept

**Capital Gravity** is a future metric representing the degree to which strategically useful public and private investment is accumulating around a region.

It must not simply total dollars. The eventual method should distinguish enabling infrastructure from expenditure needed to remediate congestion, ageing assets or other legacy problems. No formula or weighting is defined yet.

## Initial geographic coverage

The base analysis runs approximately from Brisbane / South East Queensland to Melbourne and includes inland regions plausibly part of a future urban and economic system:

- South East Queensland;
- northern New South Wales;
- New England;
- Hunter;
- Central Coast;
- Sydney;
- Central West NSW where relevant;
- Southern Highlands;
- Canberra / Yass / Goulburn;
- Riverina;
- Albury / Wodonga;
- north-east Victoria;
- Gippsland; and
- Melbourne.

Candidate discovery must not be restricted to existing rail corridors.

South Australia is excluded from the base model. A later `EXP-SA-001` should ask: **Does including Adelaide / South Australia materially improve the optimal 2075 settlement, energy, industrial or transport architecture?**
