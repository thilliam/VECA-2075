# Population layer findings v1

**Experiment:** EXP-001 east-coast base map  
**Status:** first findings from complete QLD/NSW/ACT/VIC SA2 extraction. These findings describe the inherited population system; they do not rank future settlement locations.

## Population evidence now available

VECA now has a reproducible ABS-derived population table covering **1,844 SA2s** across Queensland, New South Wales, the ACT and Victoria, with ERP observations for 2001, 2010, 2015, 2020, 2024 and 2025, plus 2024-25 natural increase, net internal migration and net overseas migration.

The separate ABS 2025 1 km population grid is retained as raw source evidence for spatial-density analysis.

Source IDs: `SRC-ABS-SA2-ERP-2025`, `SRC-ABS-POPGRID-2025`.

## Finding P1 — current rapid growth is dominated by greenfield metropolitan expansion

**Classification:** Evidence with limited inference  
**Claim:** The largest 2024-25 SA2 population gains in the four-jurisdiction dataset are overwhelmingly in outer metropolitan growth corridors around Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane.

Examples include Rockbank - Mount Cottrell (+4,602), Mickleham - Yuroke (+4,145), Fraser Rise - Plumpton (+4,064), Box Hill - Nelson (+3,911), Clyde North - South (+3,771), Marsden Park - Shanes Park (+3,172), Ripley (+3,131) and Austral - Greendale (+3,082).

**Inference:** Current population growth strongly reflects where housing and serviced greenfield land are being supplied. It must not be interpreted as evidence that these locations are intrinsically the best 2075 settlement choices.

## Finding P2 — internal migration exposes a different signal from headline growth

**Classification:** Evidence  
**Claim:** The 2024-25 component data allow VECA to distinguish domestic relocation from natural increase and overseas migration.

The largest net internal-migration gains include Box Hill - Nelson (+3,459), Marsden Park - Shanes Park (+2,617), Austral - Greendale (+2,585), Schofields - East (+1,731), Oran Park (+1,185), Leppington - Catherine Field (+1,180), Picton - Tahmoor - Buxton (+843), Branxton - Greta - Pokolbin (+807), Morisset - Cooranbong (+686), Douglas Park - Appin (+644) and Googong (+628).

**Implication:** A later revealed-attractiveness analysis should distinguish places attracting existing Australian residents from places growing primarily through overseas migration or natural increase. Even internal migration is not automatically a pure lifestyle preference signal because housing availability and affordability constrain choice.

## Finding P3 — Canberra/Queanbeyan provides a useful micro-example of spatial redistribution

**Classification:** Evidence  
**Claim:** Adjacent SA2s in the Canberra/Queanbeyan system can have radically different trajectories.

Googong grew from 937 residents in 2015 to 9,323 in 2025, an increase of 8,386. In 2024-25 alone it gained 827 residents, including net internal migration of +628.

By contrast, Queanbeyan West - Jerrabomberra fell from 13,164 in 2015 to 12,735 in 2025. In 2024-25 it lost 137 people despite natural increase of +85 and net overseas migration of +89, because net internal migration was -311.

**Inference:** A centre can appear stagnant or declining while population is relocating a short distance into newly supplied housing nearby. VECA therefore needs to analyse functional regions and settlement systems, not rank individual SA2 polygons in isolation.

## Finding P4 — regional growth is heterogeneous

**Classification:** Evidence  
**Claim:** Regional Australia is not following a single growth/decline trajectory.

Examples from the current extraction:

- Goulburn: 23,138 (2015) to 25,647 (2025), +10.84%.
- Goulburn Surrounds: 12,715 to 14,411, +13.34%.
- Jindabyne - Berridale: 7,020 to 8,703, +23.97%.
- Braidwood: 3,849 to 4,494, +16.76%.
- Cooma: 6,887 to 6,598, -4.20%.
- Mount Isa lost 143 residents in 2024-25.
- Lismore lost 293 residents in 2024-25.

**Implication:** Region type and growth driver must be researched separately. A resource town, lifestyle destination, disaster-affected centre and regional service centre can show similar headline population changes for completely different reasons.

## Finding P5 — one-year decline is not sufficient evidence of structural decline

**Classification:** Inference from evidence  
**Claim:** The largest one-year declines include places with very different economic and demographic structures: Lismore, Flemington, Mildura - North, Wagga Wagga - West, Mount Isa, Cooma, Lithgow and several established Sydney suburbs.

**Implication:** VECA should not label a location as declining or unsuitable from the 2024-25 change alone. The 5-, 10- and 24-year trajectories, demographic components, housing supply, employment change, disasters and local industry must be examined before causal classification.

## Finding P6 — SA2 and the 1 km grid answer different questions

**Classification:** Methodological inference  
**Claim:** SA2 data provide excellent population totals, trajectories and demographic components, but large SA2 polygons can conceal highly concentrated settlement footprints.

**Implication:** Use SA2 for population history and regional comparison; use the ABS 1 km grid for actual settlement density, urban continuity and later proximity calculations. Do not represent a large rural SA2 as uniformly populated.

## Next population analysis

The population source layer is sufficiently complete for EXP-001 to move to the next base-map layer. Population research will remain open for later enrichment, especially:

- functional-region aggregation rather than SA2-only interpretation;
- region-type classification;
- evidence-backed growth-driver attribution;
- age structure and workforce composition;
- 1 km grid-derived settlement footprints; and
- long-run internal-migration / revealed-attractiveness analysis.

These are analytical enrichments, not blockers to beginning the transport/infrastructure base layers.
