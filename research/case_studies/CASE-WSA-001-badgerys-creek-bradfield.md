# CASE-WSA-001 — Badgerys Creek / Western Sydney Airport / Bradfield

**Status:** must-study future case. Not part of EXP-001 scoring.

## Why this case matters

Badgerys Creek is arguably Australia's clearest current example of infrastructure-led city formation at very large scale.

The Commonwealth acquired the airport site in the 1980s. Western Sydney International is scheduled to begin freight operations in July 2026 and passenger operations on 25 October 2026. The site therefore illustrates a planning and land-reservation horizon of roughly four decades.

Around that reserved site, governments are now assembling a much larger system: international airport capacity, motorway access, a new metro line, water and recycled-water infrastructure, industrial and logistics precincts, advanced manufacturing, housing and Bradfield City.

This makes the case relevant to VECA for two distinct reasons:

1. **Option value and land reservation** — relatively early land/corridor protection can shape infrastructure choices many decades later.
2. **National spatial allocation** — when Australia decides to create a major new airport-and-city system, should that investment reinforce an existing metropolis or create an independent regional city elsewhere?

## Core counterfactual question

If Australia in the mid-1980s had known that the eventual Badgerys Creek strategy would involve tens of billions of dollars of public enabling infrastructure plus major private investment, would attaching the entire system to Sydney necessarily have been the best national spatial choice?

Possible answer: yes. Sydney provides enormous inherited population, labour, capital, institutions, markets and international economic gravity. The airport also addresses a real constraint in the existing Sydney aviation system.

But VECA should explicitly test the alternative rather than assume it away:

> existing regional centre + international airport + high-speed intercity connectivity + university/research capability + renewable energy + secure water + industry + housing → independent 300,000–500,000-person city

The objective is not to argue that Badgerys Creek was a mistake. It is to understand the trade between **reinforcing existing agglomeration** and **using a generational infrastructure package to create a new national growth pole**.

## Current scale to document

Authoritative 2026 sources report:

- Western Sydney International Airport begins passenger operations in October 2026.
- The M12 Motorway opened in March 2026 as direct airport access.
- Sydney Metro – Western Sydney Airport is a new 23 km line connecting St Marys, the airport and Bradfield; testing is expected to begin in 2026.
- The Western Sydney Aerotropolis covers approximately 11,200 hectares.
- Infrastructure NSW reports more than $28 billion in NSW/Commonwealth enabling infrastructure associated with the Aerotropolis.
- The Bradfield City Centre master plan provides capacity for about 10,000 homes and 20,000 jobs, within a wider Aerotropolis target exceeding 100,000 jobs.
- Private development proposals across the Aerotropolis are already measured in the tens of billions of dollars.

These numbers must be decomposed by project and status before any later cost comparison. Do not add headline values together without removing overlaps.

## Research questions

### Historical decision path
- Why was Badgerys Creek selected over alternatives?
- What alternatives were seriously considered in the 1970s–1990s?
- When was the land acquired, at what cost, and how much land/corridor was preserved?
- Which later development options existed because of that early reservation?
- Which options were lost or made more expensive because complementary corridors were not reserved at the same time?

### Economic gravity versus independent-city formation
- How much of the project's expected economic benefit derives specifically from proximity to the existing Sydney economy?
- How much infrastructure spending is required precisely because the site is embedded in Greater Sydney's existing urban system?
- Would a comparable airport-led regional city have required more or less total enabling infrastructure per additional resident/job?
- At what population/economic scale does an independent regional centre generate sufficient agglomeration to compete with metropolitan attachment?

### Infrastructure inheritance
- Which assets are genuinely inherited from Sydney/Western Sydney?
- Which assets had to be newly created: airport, metro, motorway, arterial roads, utilities, stormwater, recycled water, schools, health, housing and social infrastructure?
- What is the effective infrastructure inheritance per additional resident and per additional job?

### Urban-form question
- Is Bradfield becoming a genuinely polycentric/independent employment city, or primarily another node within an expanding Sydney metropolitan footprint?
- What share of future workers are expected to live locally versus commute from the wider metropolis?
- Does airport-led employment produce a sufficiently diverse city economy, or does it remain dependent on metropolitan Sydney?

### Option-value lesson for VECA
- What did reserving Badgerys Creek land in the 1980s cost relative to the strategic option it created?
- What analogous land, utility and transport corridors could Australia protect in 2026–2030 at comparatively low cost?
- Which reservations are robust across multiple future settlement scenarios even if the associated megaproject is not built for decades?

## Counterfactual comparison template

A later VECA experiment should compare Badgerys Creek with at least one plausible regional alternative using the same functional package:

- international airport;
- intercity/HSR connection;
- major road/freight connection;
- university/research presence;
- energy and transmission;
- secure water and recycling;
- industrial/logistics land;
- housing for 300,000–500,000 residents;
- health, education and social infrastructure;
- environmental and agricultural opportunity cost.

The comparison should estimate whole-system national effects, not merely airport construction cost.

## Doctrine implication

Badgerys Creek provides a concrete precedent for VECA's option-value principle:

> Land and corridor reservations made decades before demand fully materialises can preserve nationally significant choices at a fraction of the later cost of retrofitting them.

This is evidence that a 2075 planning horizon is institutionally unusual but not inherently fanciful.

## Evidence / inference discipline

**Evidence:** the site was acquired decades before airport opening; major enabling infrastructure and Bradfield development are now being delivered around it.

**Inference to test:** early reservation created very large strategic option value.

**Hypothesis to test:** a comparable package attached to an existing regional centre might, in some circumstances, create more national value than further extension of an already very large metropolis.

Do not promote the hypothesis to a recommendation without counterfactual modelling.
