# Design Principles

These principles are doctrine for VECA-2075. They exist to stop early assumptions, available datasets, current political structures or attractive technologies from silently defining the answer.

## 1. Design 2075, not 2026

Existing cities, infrastructure and political boundaries are inputs, not immutable constraints. Do not assume today's CBDs, metropolitan centres, transport corridors or population distributions remain dominant in 2075.

## 2. Settlement before transport

Do not design high-speed rail first. Determine plausible future settlement and economic patterns first, then derive the transport system those patterns require.

## 3. Optimise door-to-door systems

Do not optimise station-to-station travel in isolation. A Sydney Central terminus can be inferior to Parramatta, Western Sydney or another multimodal node if total journeys are better. CBD-centricity is not a default assumption.

## 4. Existing population is not destiny

Current population size should receive relatively little weight in future settlement potential. Otherwise the analysis will simply rediscover Brisbane, Sydney and Melbourne.

## 5. Existing investment does matter

Map infrastructure completed approximately 2015–2026, infrastructure under construction, funded or approved projects, credible planned projects and major private-sector capital investment. Existing capital creates opportunities and constraints, but sunk capital does not automatically dictate future strategy.

## 6. Separate evidence from inference from hypothesis

Every important project statement must be distinguishable as:

- **Evidence** — a sourced factual statement.
- **Inference** — a conclusion derived from stated evidence.
- **Hypothesis** — an idea to test.

Speculation must never silently become fact.

## 7. Infrastructure is a system

Transport, energy, water, digital networks, ports, freight, health, education and industry interact. Analyse their combined spatial effects rather than treating each project category independently.

## 8. Use future climate

Settlement suitability must use plausible 2050–2075 climate and hazard conditions where evidence permits, not today's climate alone.

## 9. Water is an engineering-economic variable

Do not reduce water suitability to historic rainfall or nearby rivers. Consider catchments, reservoirs, groundwater, recycling, desalination, pipelines, pumping energy, resilience and cost per resident. Conversely, do not assume desalination makes every location equally viable.

## 10. Energy geography matters

Future settlements and energy-intensive industry may benefit from proximity to renewable resources, storage and transmission. Do not assume all generation and transmission should follow historic population geography.

## 11. Agriculture and ecology have value

Cheap land is not equivalent to developable land. Explicitly account for high-value agriculture, protected environments, biodiversity, Indigenous cultural values, bushfire, flood, terrain, soil and geology.

## 12. Optimise the whole country, not individual projects

A project can be locally rational and nationally suboptimal. VECA seeks a coherent long-term spatial system, not a collection of individually justified projects.

## 13. Preserve optionality

Favour corridor and land reservation, staged commitments and other choices that preserve future options under uncertainty. A low-cost decision in 2030 may preserve an option worth tens of billions in 2050.

## 14. Avoid false precision

Early scores, maps and cost estimates are exploratory. They are not engineering-grade forecasts. Record uncertainty and confidence rather than disguising them with excessive numerical precision.

## 15. Actively search for surprising answers

The analysis must be able to show that current intuitions are wrong. Do not construct methods merely to validate New England, Canberra, Albury, Parramatta or any other favoured hypothesis.
