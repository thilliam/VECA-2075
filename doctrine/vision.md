# Vision

## Vision East Coast Australia 2075

VECA-2075 asks what eastern Australia could become if major settlement and infrastructure choices were made as parts of one 50-year system rather than as disconnected responses to near-term pressure.

The project starts from a simple observation: Australia will spend enormous amounts on housing, transport, energy, water, health, education, industry and urban infrastructure between now and 2075. The central question is therefore not whether to invest. It is whether the geography and sequencing of that investment can produce a substantially better national outcome.

## The opportunity

Australia has deliberately planned settlement before. Canberra is the obvious example. The tools available now are dramatically more capable: high-resolution spatial data, future-climate modelling, modern water engineering, renewable generation and storage, computational optimisation and AI-assisted research.

VECA explores whether those capabilities allow a modern, continental-scale approach to settlement planning.

## What success would mean

A successful VECA investigation would identify plausible spatial systems that can accommodate major population growth while improving, rather than merely preserving:

- quality of life and housing affordability;
- access to productive employment;
- infrastructure efficiency;
- water and energy security;
- resilience to future climate and natural hazards;
- national freight and passenger movement;
- environmental and agricultural outcomes; and
- long-term strategic optionality.

The output is not expected to be one perfect master plan. It should expose trade-offs, identify robust choices, preserve options under uncertainty and show where conventional assumptions fail.

## Initial forcing scenario

The initial investigation asks how eastern Australia might accommodate roughly **10 million additional residents**. This is deliberately a scenario, not a population forecast. It is large enough to force meaningful choices about settlement geography and infrastructure.

## Settlement before transport

VECA does not begin with high-speed rail, motorways, airports or any other preferred technology. It first asks where future population and productive activity should be located. Transport is then derived as part of the system required to connect those places.

## Geographic starting point

The base study covers eastern Australia from South East Queensland to Melbourne, including inland regions that could plausibly participate in a future urban and economic network. South Australia is initially excluded so the first model remains bounded. A later experiment will test whether including Adelaide and South Australia materially improves the overall architecture.

## Intellectual standard

VECA should be capable of surprising us. A model that merely rediscovers today's largest cities, or validates locations we already favour, has failed its most important test.
