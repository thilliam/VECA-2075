# EXP-001 cross-domain synthesis v1

**Status:** exploratory synthesis. This is not a settlement ranking and does not nominate future cities or an HSR alignment.

## What is now visible

The inherited east-coast system is not simply three capital cities connected by transport. Several different forms of spatial gravity appear in the evidence:

### 1. Metropolitan capital accumulation

SEQ, Western Sydney and Melbourne continue to absorb enormous public transport/road/water investment. This reinforces current metropolitan systems, but some expenditure is capacity-expansion while some is remediation of congestion or legacy constraints. VECA must not treat every dollar equally.

### 2. Deliberately created non-CBD nodes

Western Sydney Airport/Aerotropolis/Bradfield is the clearest contemporary example: airport, metro, motorway, water, industrial land, advanced manufacturing and private logistics investment are being created together. This is evidence that governments can manufacture new economic gravity at very large scale.

### 3. Inland logistics/industry nodes

Parkes and Wagga Wagga show a different model. Their resident populations are much smaller, but governments are deliberately combining serviced industrial land with national rail/highway logistics. Wagga adds an established regional-city services base, new intermodal infrastructure and comparatively strong water-treatment inheritance.

### 4. Emerging energy nodes

New England and Central-West Orana are being assigned multi-gigawatt transmission/REZ roles that are large relative to their present populations. HumeLink, VNI West, Hunter transmission and Queensland reinforcements also change the future geography of grid accessibility.

### 5. Water-system inheritance varies structurally

Melbourne and SEQ increasingly obtain resilience through interconnected grids plus climate-independent/recycled sources. Canberra-Queanbeyan is already a cross-border multi-source system. Wagga and Albury sit on large regulated rivers and have treatment infrastructure, while smaller inland systems such as Goulburn have much smaller local storages.

## First cross-domain observations

**Evidence:** Wagga Wagga combines regional population scale, an operating/expanding water system, Inland Rail corridor position, RiFL intermodal infrastructure, a Special Activation Precinct and proximity to HumeLink.

**Inference:** this combination is more strategically meaningful than any one of those assets in isolation. It warrants later candidate-region analysis, but does not establish preferred settlement status.

**Evidence:** Parkes combines nationally unusual rail geometry and serviced industrial land, but has a much smaller existing urban/service base.

**Inference:** Parkes is a useful test of whether infrastructure position alone can overcome limited city scale, services and water constraints.

**Evidence:** New England is receiving planned energy-network capacity far out of proportion to current population.

**Inference:** the region should be tested as an energy-enabled future settlement/industry system rather than evaluated only as today's Armidale/Tamworth economy.

**Evidence:** Western Sydney demonstrates that tens of billions of enabling infrastructure plus planning reform can attract large private projects.

**Inference:** VECA should compare the opportunity cost of attaching future mega-investment to existing metros versus deliberately using comparable capital to create stronger independent regional centres.

## What EXP-001 still lacks

Before inherited-system synthesis can close:

- asset-level generation/storage extraction;
- broader water coverage for Toowoomba/Darling Downs, New England, Hunter and coastal regional systems;
- broader industry/logistics nodes in QLD, Hunter, Victoria and ACT;
- port catchment detail;
- a cleaner, non-double-counted recent/committed capital register;
- first rendered multilayer map.

These gaps should not prevent the next research stage from beginning because the major inherited-system structures are now sufficiently visible.
