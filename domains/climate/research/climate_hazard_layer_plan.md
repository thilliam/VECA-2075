# Habitat and climate-hazard layer plan

## Purpose

Add the future physical constraints that EXP-001 deliberately omitted: climate, flood, bushfire and other hazards that may change the desirability or cost of settlement by 2050-2075.

## Core distinction

Separate:

1. baseline climate and terrain;
2. projected climate change;
3. acute hazards (flood, bushfire, cyclone, coastal inundation);
4. chronic stresses (heat, drought, soil moisture decline, water-yield decline);
5. engineering mitigability and cost.

A hazard is not automatically an exclusion. VECA should distinguish avoidable local exposure from region-wide structural disadvantage.

## Projection horizons

Prefer datasets centred on 2050 and 2070 where available. Do not use today's climate alone and do not treat one emissions scenario as certain.

## Initial variables

- mean and extreme temperature change;
- extreme-heat frequency;
- seasonal rainfall direction/range;
- potential evapotranspiration;
- drought/time-in-drought;
- soil moisture;
- fire weather;
- extreme rainfall intensity;
- floodplain/flood-study coverage;
- coastal inundation/erosion where relevant;
- tropical cyclone exposure for northern end of study area.

## Primary national sources

CSIRO/BOM Climate Change in Australia provides projections across 20-year periods centred on 2030, 2050, 2070 and 2090. Geoscience Australia provides national flood-information, design-rainfall/flood guidance, elevation and Digital Earth Australia products.

## Guardrails

- Long-range seasonal forecasts are not climate projections.
- Flood history is not equivalent to future design flood risk.
- Fire detections are not a future bushfire-hazard model.
- Regional projection uncertainty must remain visible.
- Local topography and drainage can dominate regional averages.
- No single composite 'climate score' until variable sensitivity has been tested.
