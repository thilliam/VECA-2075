# East-coast port catchment findings — 2026-09-09

## Purpose

Treat ports as freight/economic systems rather than waterfront points. For VECA the relevant inherited capability is the combination of maritime capacity, landside rail/intermodal access, industrial land, current trade role, expansion optionality and the geography of the hinterland served.

## Findings

- **Port of Brisbane** is growing rapidly: FY25 reached 1.62m TEU and 34.9 Mt total trade, with around 200 ha of Future Port Expansion land. Its weak point is landside rail: only 2.1% of containers moved by rail in FY25. Vision 2060 projects roughly 5m+ TEU annually, making dedicated freight rail a strategic capacity issue rather than a minor transport-mode preference.
- **Port Botany** already has substantial latent maritime/container capacity: around 2.8m TEU current throughput versus more than 7m TEU stated capacity. The binding future question is increasingly landside movement. All three container terminals have on-dock rail and NSW Ports is targeting 3m TEU/year by rail. Moorebank, Enfield and Cooks River therefore belong in the same functional port-catchment system.
- **Port Kembla** should not be represented as an operating second container port today. Its value is current bulk/RoRo/project-cargo capability plus concept-approved future container optionality. The strength of that future role depends materially on freight links such as Maldon-Dombarton/SWIRL and other Western Sydney connections.
- **Port of Newcastle** has unusually large physical spare-capacity signals: the operator states capacity for more than 200 Mt of cargo and 10,000 vessel movements annually, with the shipping channel operating at about half capacity and substantial port land available. That is inherited optionality, but it does not by itself prove a viable container-market role.
- **Port of Melbourne** is already Australia's largest container port by throughput in this set (3.39m TEU FY25; 3.5m TEU CY2025). Its Port Rail Transformation Project is operational, while the next major container-capacity step is Webb Dock North, targeted around 2036 subject to approvals. Completed Swanson rail works and future Webb Dock rail access must remain separate statuses.
- **Port of Geelong** is a major regional bulk/industrial gateway, handling more than 13.6 Mt annually. It is strategically important to south-west Victoria but should not be collapsed into the same role as Melbourne's container gateway.

## VECA implication

Port capacity cannot be scored as one number. At minimum keep visible:

1. current maritime/terminal throughput;
2. declared physical/terminal capacity where published;
3. rail/intermodal access and train capability;
4. road dependence;
5. industrial/logistics land;
6. hinterland/catchment geography;
7. committed expansion;
8. preserved future optionality.

A region near a port with spare berth/channel capacity can still face a poor freight proposition if the landside network is constrained. Conversely, a mature port with strong intermodal links may possess much more usable inherited capacity than its waterfront footprint suggests.
