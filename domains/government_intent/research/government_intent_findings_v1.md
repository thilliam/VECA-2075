# Government Intent / Future Capital Optionality — findings v1

## Evidence

### 1. Governments already plan growth geography, not just projects

- NSW regional plans are 20-year place-based strategies that identify where and when growth should occur and align housing, jobs and infrastructure. They are reviewed every five years.
- The final Sydney Plan assumes Greater Sydney grows from about 5.3 million to 6.6 million people by 2046, with at least 800,000 more homes and 950,000 more jobs.
- Queensland's ShapingSEQ 2023 assumes around 6 million people by 2046, requiring almost 900,000 new homes and almost one million new jobs from the 2021 baseline. The plan includes an urban footprint, potential future growth areas, industrial/economic areas and a strategic public transport network to 2046.
- From 2026 Queensland is pairing statutory regional plans with regional infrastructure plans so land use and transport, water, energy, digital and social/community infrastructure are planned together.
- Victoria's Plan for Victoria sets explicit 2051 housing targets for every LGA and says planning schemes and future infrastructure/service business cases are to be consistent with those targets.
- ACT district strategies guide growth to 2038 and beyond to 2050 and explicitly inform future infrastructure, services and rezoning.

## 2. Current government baselines encode strong path dependency

A large share of future infrastructure demand is not independent of settlement geography. It is generated by the population and housing distribution assumed by current plans.

Examples:

- Sydney Plan infrastructure demand is partly conditional on 1.3 million additional residents remaining within Greater Sydney by 2046.
- ShapingSEQ's future transport and service requirements are partly conditional on growth to around 6 million in SEQ.
- Victoria's future service/business-case planning is explicitly tied to its 2051 LGA housing targets.

This means VECA should treat portions of future metropolitan capital as **growth-triggered**, not as an unavoidable national requirement.

## 3. Health infrastructure provides direct evidence that public capital can reshape settlement

The clearest current examples are not theoretical.

- Queensland's New Toowoomba Hospital relocates existing hospital services to the Baillie Henderson campus, consolidating the region's main referral hospital around a new health/knowledge precinct. The main hospital will have at least 538 overnight beds and Stage 1 is due to open in 2029.
- NSW is pairing the new Shellharbour Hospital precinct with rezoning proposals intended to support higher-density housing, employment and a mixed-use city centre around the health investment.
- Victoria has reorganised public health delivery into 12 Local Health Service Networks, with network-wide clinical planning and a role-delineation framework that can change what level of service different hospital sites provide.

The implication is important: a hospital is not only infrastructure that follows population. In some circumstances it is an **anchor asset that can help create a new centre of gravity**.

## 4. Renewal liability is analytically different from new capital

When a hospital, school, treatment plant or other major asset will need replacement or major refurbishment regardless of VECA, the relevant incremental cost of moving or consolidating that service may be much smaller than the headline replacement cost.

Future work should therefore distinguish:

- replacement on same site;
- major refurbishment on same site;
- expansion because of local growth;
- relocation/consolidation;
- wholly new service capacity.

Asset age alone is not enough evidence; service plans, condition assessments, master plans and business cases are needed.

## 5. Government land and corridor reservations deserve their own optionality treatment

Plans already preserve future growth areas, transport corridors, industrial precincts and public land. These assets may have high strategic value precisely because no irreversible project has yet been chosen.

VECA should value **preserved choice**, not only constructed assets.

## Inference

The most useful future economic comparison is unlikely to be:

> cost of regional city versus zero.

It should be:

> incremental cost of alternative settlement architecture versus the metropolitan/regional capital program that would otherwise be required under current government population assumptions.

This creates a new VECA quantity: **redirectable future capital**.

## Hypothesis to test

Some regional settlement strategies may appear prohibitively expensive when compared with today's infrastructure, but become substantially more competitive when compared with the future replacement, augmentation and congestion-remediation spending that governments already expect to undertake elsewhere.
