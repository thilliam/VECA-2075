# Government Intent — education asset findings v1

**Status:** exploratory evidence layer. No settlement ranking authorised.

## Why education needs separate treatment

Schools, universities and vocational/trade colleges behave differently as settlement assets.

- **Schools** are predominantly population-following infrastructure. They are a major recurring cost of growth and therefore useful for measuring growth-triggered capital, but an existing school is rarely sufficient evidence for selecting a future major settlement.
- **Universities** can be population-shaping anchors. They create research, professional employment, student migration, health partnerships, innovation capacity and cultural depth.
- **TAFE/VET/trade colleges** sit between the two. They are essential to the practical scalability of a settlement because a city that grows rapidly needs construction, electrical, energy, health, logistics, maintenance and service workforces.

VECA should therefore not combine all education into a single `education score`.

## Regional observations

### Central Coast

The Central Coast now has a distributed higher-education and vocational structure: Gosford Central, the established Ourimbah university campus, and TAFE investment across Gosford/Ourimbah/Wyong. This reinforces the emerging Gosford–Ourimbah–Wyong service corridor.

The important future question is not whether the region has education assets—it does—but whether those assets can scale to support a substantially larger independent regional economy rather than primarily serving a commuter-linked population.

### New England

Armidale is unusually education-rich relative to its population because of UNE. That makes current population a particularly poor proxy for institutional capacity here.

UNE Tamworth Central creates a complementary pattern: Armidale retains deep university/research identity while Tamworth gains a stronger CBD tertiary presence alongside health, aviation and regional services.

This makes the Armidale–Tamworth relationship a candidate for later **networked-city** testing rather than forcing VECA to assume only one can be a major future centre.

### Canberra

Canberra has an exceptional concentration of higher education, research and public-sector knowledge assets through ANU, University of Canberra and other institutions, plus a major vocational system through CIT.

This is already a mature anchor cluster. The VECA question is therefore less `can Canberra support a knowledge economy?` and more `what additional settlement geography can this existing institutional gravity support, including across the NSW border?`

### Wagga / Albury-Wodonga

Charles Sturt University plus TAFE gives both Wagga and Albury-Wodonga substantial workforce formation capacity.

Wagga is particularly interesting because university, health, logistics/SAP, water and energy-network evidence are accumulating in the same broad functional area.

Albury-Wodonga has the additional advantage of a cross-border labour market and university/TAFE/health combination. Its institutional geography should be evaluated as one functional city even where state administration is split.

### Gippsland

Federation University and TAFE Gippsland should be analysed as part of the Latrobe Valley's economic transition infrastructure, not simply as education providers.

A key later question is whether education and training capability can be aligned with new energy, advanced manufacturing and health demand strongly enough to replace declining legacy-industry employment and support larger settlement.

### SEQ / Darling Downs

Toowoomba has a meaningful university + TAFE base before any VECA intervention. This materially lowers the institutional start-up burden of larger future settlement.

Ipswich is an especially clear government-intent example: ShapingSEQ explicitly links the hospital–university precinct to western growth. Education is therefore already being used as city-shaping infrastructure rather than merely following population.

## School-capital finding

The first school pass shows why VECA should avoid attempting to catalogue every existing school at this stage.

For 2075 settlement analysis the more valuable objects are:

1. **new-school reservations** in growth areas;
2. **committed new schools and major expansions**;
3. **school-capacity pressure** generated by official housing/population targets;
4. **cost norms** for providing primary/secondary capacity per additional resident or dwelling.

This converts schools from thousands of map points into a measurable **growth liability / capital requirement**.

## New analytical object — Human Capital Formation Capacity

The anchor-asset work suggests a useful later metric family:

> **Human Capital Formation Capacity:** how readily can a region educate and train the professional, technical, trade and service workforce required for substantial additional settlement and economic activity?

Keep its components visible:

- university/research depth;
- health/clinical training;
- trades/VET capacity;
- school capacity and expansion pathway;
- specialist workforce pipelines;
- ability to attract students/workers from outside the region.

Do not reduce this to a single score yet.

## Proposed next target — service-capacity join

The next target should **not** be another standalone asset catalogue.

We now have enough parallel layers to begin a first **regional service-capacity join** for the six study regions:

- health anchors;
- universities and VET;
- school-growth liability;
- government land/growth areas;
- water systems;
- energy/transmission inheritance;
- road/rail/freight/airport accessibility;
- industry/logistics nodes;
- official population/housing intent.

The output should be a `regional_anchor_clusters_v1` dataset that answers, without ranking:

> What useful systems already co-exist within each regional catchment, what is missing, and which missing components would require genuinely new capital rather than capital governments are likely to spend anyway?

This is the bridge from evidence collection to EXP-002 candidate discovery.

### Why this target now

Continuing to collect individual assets indefinitely will produce completeness without insight. The VECA hypothesis depends on **combinations**: hospital + university + trade training + water + grid + developable land + logistics may make a region structurally different from one possessing only one or two of those assets.

The join should remain descriptive. No weighted ranking or preferred settlement is authorised until climate/terrain/land gates are sufficiently mature.
