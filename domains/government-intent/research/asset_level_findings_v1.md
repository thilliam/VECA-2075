# Asset-level government intent findings v1

## Scope

This pass examines six VECA regions at anchor-asset level: Central Coast, New England, Canberra, Wagga/Albury, Gippsland and South East Queensland (SEQ). It focuses on health, tertiary education, vocational education and government-controlled land that can materially shape settlement geography.

The purpose is not to rate regions. It is to distinguish **fixed inherited gravity**, **new committed anchors**, **service-role optionality**, and **land that still preserves a spatial choice**.

## Evidence findings

### Central Coast — increasingly polycentric but strongly pre-shaped

- Wyong Hospital's $200 million redevelopment was completed in late 2025, materially reinforcing the northern Central Coast health node.
- Gosford now combines a major hospital, University of Newcastle clinical education, and the new Gosford Central university building. The new campus was jointly funded by university, Commonwealth and NSW governments on NSW Government development-corporation land.
- TAFE NSW is investing $38.3 million across Gosford, Ourimbah and Wyong.
- NSW strategic conservation planning is intended to unlock roughly 14,000 homes and 30,000 jobs in areas including Somersby, Greater Warnervale and Greater Lake Munmorah while resolving environmental constraints earlier in the process.

**Inference:** Central Coast government intent is not simply 'more Gosford'. It is reinforcing a north-central-south network of established service centres, with Gosford as the strongest knowledge/health node and Warnervale/Wyong as a major growth/service axis.

### New England — Armidale and Tamworth are deliberately differentiated

- The 2041 regional plan explicitly describes Armidale as the region's higher-education heart and Tamworth as the predominant aviation, health and employment centre.
- UNE remains Armidale's defining knowledge institution, while a new UNE Tamworth Central campus is under construction with NSW Government funding and other contributions.
- Moree, Gunnedah and Glen Innes all have current hospital capital programs; Moree also has Special Activation Precinct investment.
- A 16.5 ha East Tamworth government-land parcel, including former TAFE land, is being rezoned for housing after identification through the statewide land audit.

**Inference:** The state is already building a complementary two-city structure: Armidale as university/knowledge anchor and Tamworth as broader health/employment/service centre. VECA should test whether future transport and settlement strategy strengthens this complementarity rather than forcing one city to replicate the other.

### Canberra — long-term planning is unusually integrated

- ACT district strategies explicitly connect growth with services, transport, employment, health, education and nature toward 2038 and beyond 2050.
- The Southern Gateway framework integrates Canberra Hospital, Woden town centre and future North Woden development within a 2050+ corridor framework.
- A new Northside Hospital on the North Canberra Hospital campus is now committed, making the northside hospital site progressively less spatially flexible.

**Inference:** Canberra provides one of the clearest Australian examples of service infrastructure and land-use planning being designed together. For VECA, Canberra-Queanbeyan should be analysed as a functional cross-border city-region, not merely an ACT jurisdiction.

### Wagga / Albury — Wagga is inherited; Albury-Wodonga still exposes a contested choice

- Wagga Wagga Health Service's major redevelopment was completed in 2021. That capital is now inherited rather than redirectable.
- Albury-Wodonga's $558 million hospital redevelopment is actively consolidating complex care on the Albury campus, with early works under construction and the main Clinical Services Building progressing through planning.
- Local governments continue to advocate for a fully funded greenfield single-site hospital, demonstrating that the spatial choice remains politically contested even as capital lock-in increases.
- The Wodonga campus is expected to retain a future non-emergency-care role, showing that service functions can remain spatially flexible after the major acute-care site becomes fixed.
- Wagga and Albury both retain significant TAFE infrastructure alongside Charles Sturt University and other tertiary assets.

**Inference:** Albury-Wodonga is an unusually valuable VECA case study because it demonstrates the exact transition from **high optionality -> contested choice -> committed site lock-in** in real time.

### Gippsland — Latrobe Valley service gravity is compounding

- Latrobe Regional Hospital has received repeated major expansion, including the $223.5 million Stage 3A program.
- Federation University and TAFE Gippsland form a regional tertiary/vocational system, with current strategy focused on substantially increasing locally trained graduates through 2050.

**Inference:** Traralgon/Churchill/greater Latrobe should be treated as an emerging integrated health-education-workforce system rather than three unrelated towns. Existing service concentration may become highly valuable if other EXP-002 constraints are favourable.

### SEQ — government land and planned growth are actively manufacturing geography

- ShapingSEQ explicitly identifies the Ipswich hospital-University of Southern Queensland knowledge and technology precinct as critical to Western sub-region growth.
- Ripley Valley, Springfield and Rosewood-Thagoona-Walloon are major planned expansion areas; Toowoomba, Highfields, Westbrook and Drayton are also identified for growth.
- Queensland's Land Activation Program is now using government-controlled sites and priority-development-area powers to fast-track more than 5,000 homes and mixed-use precincts in Brisbane and Logan.
- New Toowoomba Hospital is a strong example of hospital renewal being used to create a new integrated health/knowledge campus rather than simply rebuilding the old facility in place.

**Inference:** SEQ provides direct proof that **government-controlled land + zoning + anchor services + transport** can be used as a coordinated settlement tool. VECA's question is therefore not whether this is possible, but whether such tools should be used to reinforce today's metropolitan pattern or deliberately create stronger regional alternatives.

## Cross-domain finding

A new analytical object is needed: the **Anchor Asset Cluster**.

A major hospital, university, TAFE, government development land and transport node within a coherent catchment can have much more settlement value together than the sum of their individual capital costs.

Future candidate analysis should therefore capture both:

1. individual asset lock-in / optionality; and
2. cluster effects within plausible 15-, 30- and 60-minute accessibility bands.
