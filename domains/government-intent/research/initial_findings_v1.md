# Government intent / future capital optionality — initial findings v1

## Purpose

This domain asks a different question from the inherited-infrastructure map:

> What future spending, zoning and service geography is already implied by current government plans, and how much of that future capital is still movable before it becomes sunk?

The objective is not to second-guess every agency project. It is to expose decision windows where VECA's alternative settlement architecture could change where inevitable future expenditure occurs.

## 1. Government regional plans are explicit spatial baselines

NSW regional plans are particularly useful because they state where growth should occur and couple this with infrastructure sequencing, employment land, servicing and environmental constraints. Hunter, Central Coast, New England North West, Riverina Murray and Central West/Orana should therefore be treated as the current NSW Government baseline against which VECA alternatives are tested.

Queensland's ShapingSEQ assumes growth from about 3.8 million to 6 million people by 2046. This is not merely a demographic forecast: it implies large additional transport, water, health, education and utility expenditure across SEQ. VECA needs to separate capital required to renew existing SEQ assets from capital triggered specifically by accommodating that additional population in SEQ.

Victoria's regional planning framework remains structurally useful even where the underlying growth plans are older. Current planning provisions still direct growth toward Shepparton, Wangaratta, Wodonga and Benalla, strengthen Seymour, and seek to coordinate regional settlement with infrastructure and employment. The Hume plan also explicitly contemplated redirecting some growth away from Melbourne.

## 2. Health capital is a settlement anchor and a lock-in mechanism

John Hunter Health and Innovation Precinct is the strongest first example. The $890 million expansion is nearly complete and is intended to serve the Hunter and northern NSW as a tertiary referral centre. For VECA this means Newcastle's tertiary-health role should now be considered inherited capital with very low redirectability.

That is different from New England North West, where Moree and Glen Innes district hospitals are being renewed while specialist services remain networked to Armidale and Tamworth. These investments improve local service resilience but leave substantially more freedom over the future geography of higher-order specialist health capacity.

Toowoomba provides an important process example: a large regional hospital decision that might once have represented future-capital optionality has now moved into committed delivery. VECA should try to identify equivalent decisions *before* they cross that threshold.

## 3. Regional health networks reveal hierarchy, not just hospital dots

A district hospital can be modern while still depending on a regional tertiary hub. Therefore the health layer should record:

- service level / referral role;
- network relationships;
- travel catchment;
- workforce constraints;
- age/condition where available;
- committed capital;
- likely next major renewal window.

A future settlement strategy should ask whether higher-order capacity could be placed where it strengthens an intended regional network rather than automatically expanding the incumbent tertiary site.

## 4. Four early capital-optionality archetypes

### Hunter — increasingly locked-in regional anchor
Major tertiary health, motorway, rail and energy investments reinforce Newcastle/Hunter. VECA should not treat this as freely movable future capital. The question becomes how much *additional* growth should exploit that inheritance versus whether further concentration creates diminishing returns.

### New England — energy-first, services still comparatively open
Major renewable/transmission investment is arriving, while health geography remains more distributed and lower-order. This creates a potentially valuable decision window: if large future population growth were ever intended for the region, higher-order health/education infrastructure has not yet been committed at metropolitan scale.

### Riverina / Hume — cross-border system opportunity
Wagga, Albury-Wodonga, Shepparton and Wodonga sit within overlapping NSW/Victorian planning systems. Water, freight, health and settlement planning often cross the border functionally while funding and regulation remain jurisdictional. VECA should treat cross-border coordination itself as an infrastructure/productivity opportunity.

### SEQ — very large growth-triggered capital pool
ShapingSEQ's 6 million population assumption implies enormous future expenditure. This is likely the single biggest counterfactual capital pool for VECA: how much of the transport, health, education, water and housing-enabling infrastructure required for an additional ~2.2 million SEQ residents could be avoided or redirected under a more distributed east-coast settlement system?

## 5. Next extraction priorities

1. Facility-by-facility hospital pipeline for SEQ and Darling Downs, including project maturity.
2. NSW regional tertiary/service hierarchy for Hunter, New England, Central West/Orana, Riverina and Central Coast.
3. Victorian major regional hospital/service networks, especially Albury-Wodonga, Shepparton, Bendigo, Ballarat and Gippsland.
4. Education/university capital and major school-growth programs.
5. Zoning/growth-area land releases and enabling-infrastructure assumptions.
6. State-owned land/corridor reservations.
7. Asset-age/renewal indicators where published.

## Guardrail

`redirectable` does not mean a government should cancel a project. It means the underlying future need is not yet geographically irreversible. VECA should never count committed expenditure twice or assume that all renewal capital could be freely relocated.
