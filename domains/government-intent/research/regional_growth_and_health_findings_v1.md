# Regional growth and health-capital findings v1

## Purpose

Turn current government plans into VECA evidence about assumed future geography and the flexibility of future public capital.

## Evidence

### NSW regional planning is explicitly infrastructure-sequenced

The Hunter Regional Plan 2041 assumes growth from about 860,000 to nearly 950,000 people and 101,800 additional dwellings. The Central Coast plan assumes growth from about 347,500 to 404,250 and 32,550 additional dwellings. Central West and Orana projects 291,073 to 324,943 people and at least 21,664 additional homes.

The Central Coast implementation model is especially important for VECA: the state says the capacity, cost, timing and take-up of infrastructure will influence where new homes and jobs should be located. That is effectively a government version of one part of VECA's optimisation problem.

### SEQ has unusually explicit spatial commitments

ShapingSEQ 2023 assumes roughly 3.8 million people growing to about 6 million by 2046 and 863,800 additional dwellings from the 2021 baseline. It seeks at least 60/40 consolidation versus expansion, moving toward 70/30, and requires land-use and infrastructure sequencing.

It also preserves Potential Future Growth Areas outside the current Urban Footprint. These are not development commitments. This is highly relevant to VECA because they represent explicit spatial optionality already held by government.

Toowoomba is particularly important: the 2046 plan carries roughly 31,300 additional dwellings in the urban extent and identifies future-growth/development areas around Highfields, Westbrook, Wellcamp and West Toowoomba. This should be tested as an inland growth architecture rather than treated as merely an appendage to SEQ.

### Riverina requires cross-border treatment

The Riverina Murray plan explicitly notes that Albury-Wodonga and other border communities operate across the NSW/Victoria border, including housing markets, commuting, freight and service relationships. VECA should therefore model these as functional regions rather than jurisdictional fragments.

### Victoria's planning stack has a freshness problem

Victoria now has a statewide Plan for Victoria to 2050, but many regional growth frameworks still derive from 2013-14 plans. These older plans remain useful for spatial intent, transport reservations and centre hierarchies, but should not be mistaken for current growth forecasts. VECA needs to reconcile the 2050 statewide plan with Hume, Gippsland and other legacy regional frameworks.

## Health-capital implications

Queensland currently provides the clearest evidence of health capital as both renewal and geography-shaping infrastructure. Its Hospital Rescue Plan includes new hospitals, expansions and a reported maintenance backlog. The New Toowoomba Hospital is a concrete precedent for relocating major hospital functions and using the new site as a broader health/knowledge precinct.

Victoria's current reform is analytically even more interesting: Local Health Service Networks and a Role Delineation Framework are explicitly reconsidering which sites perform which functions. This suggests that some future hospital capital should be treated as a service-network allocation problem, not simply a building-replacement problem.

NSW's Regional Health Strategic Plan provides the statewide service-policy layer but is not yet enough to identify individual replacement horizons. A deeper asset-by-asset pass is still required.

## Inferences

1. **Government growth forecasts are endogenous.** They are partly consequences of zoning, infrastructure sequencing and land release, not neutral predictions of where Australians naturally want to live.
2. **Renewal liability is a real VECA asset class.** Maintenance backlogs, masterplans and ageing hospitals imply future spending that may be unavoidable even if its location is not.
3. **Preserved growth areas are option value.** PFGAs, development areas and reserved corridors should be mapped separately from committed construction.
4. **Health infrastructure can be an anchor.** A tertiary hospital, university and research precinct may create settlement gravity rather than merely respond to it.
5. **Cross-border regions expose planning inefficiency.** Albury-Wodonga is the clearest current example; Canberra-Queanbeyan has similar implications in water and services.

## Next extraction targets

- hospital/site masterplans and major redevelopment horizons for Hunter/Newcastle, Central Coast, New England, Wagga, Albury-Wodonga, Canberra, Gippsland/Latrobe and major SEQ regional centres;
- school capacity / new-school planning where spatially explicit;
- TAFE/university campus expansion and health-education precincts;
- government-owned land, urban release areas and priority development areas;
- long-horizon water, wastewater and electricity distribution augmentation tied to these growth assumptions;
- local government structure plans only where they materially change the state-level picture.
