# Land, terrain and ecology layer plan

## Purpose

Identify where physical land conditions, existing productive use, protected areas and legal/cultural constraints materially affect the feasibility or desirability of future settlement.

## Initial layers

- elevation and slope;
- broad terrain/earthworks implications;
- current land use;
- high-value/intensive agriculture;
- protected areas and conservation estate;
- native-title determinations and Indigenous Land Use Agreements as a consultation/legal-context layer;
- urban/industrial land already transformed;
- mining/resource land where relevant;
- floodplain/wetland overlap from the hazard layer.

## Primary sources

- Geoscience Australia national 1-second DEM (2024 service)
- ABARES Land use of Australia 2010-11 to 2020-21 v7.1 (published Aug 2026)
- ABARES catchment-scale land use update Dec 2023 v2 for local detail
- DCCEEW CAPAD 2024 protected-area spatial data
- National Native Title Tribunal spatial datasets, refreshed daily

## Guardrails

- Cheap/open land is not automatically developable.
- Agricultural value must not be approximated from land price alone.
- Protected-area and native-title layers have different legal meanings and must not be collapsed into one exclusion mask.
- Native title / ILUA presence is not an automatic prohibition on development; it signals rights, agreements, consultation and legal context requiring proper treatment.
- Slope is a construction-cost driver, not a categorical exclusion until thresholds are tested.
- Current land use is inherited evidence, not destiny.

## Later derived questions

- How much low-slope land surrounds each functional regional centre after protected areas, flood constraints and high-value agriculture are made visible?
- Which apparent greenfield opportunities require destroying unusually valuable agricultural land?
- Which corridors face terrain penalties large enough to change transport/utility economics?
- Where can urban expansion reuse already-disturbed land or lower-conflict land?
