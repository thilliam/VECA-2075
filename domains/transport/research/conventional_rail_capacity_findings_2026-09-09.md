# Conventional rail capacity and investment — first structured pass

**Date:** 9 September 2026  
**Status:** current operator/program evidence; first capacity/investment layer, not yet a full route-access extraction.

## Why this changes the VECA transport picture

The existing rail geometry layer tells us where railway exists. It does not tell us whether a corridor can carry a long/heavy train, how mixed passenger/freight traffic interacts, where overtaking is possible, or where governments/operators are already spending to remove constraints.

The first capacity/investment pass therefore separates:

- physical corridor existence;
- operating capability;
- active capacity/resilience investment;
- completed capability upgrades;
- funded programs with project scope still unresolved;
- corridor preservation without delivery commitment.

## Existing conventional rail is receiving major new capital

ARTC's Network Investment Program commits almost **A$2.8 billion** through Horizons 1 and 2:

- Horizon 1: **A$1.04 billion** beginning 2024–25;
- Horizon 2: **A$1.75 billion** over six years, announced in the May 2026 Federal Budget.

The east-coast corridors explicitly affected include Albury–Sydney, Melbourne–Albury and Telarah–Acacia Ridge. Horizon 1 work includes track rehabilitation and culvert upgrades on Albury–Sydney, while Horizon 2 project details remain subject to business cases.

For VECA, this means the counterfactual is not `existing conventional rail stays frozen while a new network is built`. The inherited network itself is being renewed and selectively expanded.

## Mixed traffic is a real capacity problem

ARTC describes the Southern Highlands as its most heavily used corridor outside the Hunter Valley. The A$60 million Werai/Joppa Junction overtaking project is designed to let freight trains up to **1,800 metres** long be overtaken by faster passenger services.

That is a useful example of a comparatively modest intervention improving throughput, reliability and passenger/freight coexistence without building a new intercity route.

Later VECA transport comparisons should therefore include passing opportunities, duplication, signalling and timetable flexibility—not only maximum line speed.

## Inland Rail status is spatially uneven

The current program must not be represented as one continuous `under construction Melbourne–Brisbane` line.

As of September 2026:

- construction between **Beveridge and Parkes** remains the delivery priority;
- Stockinbingal–Parkes and Parkes–Narromine are completed/operational;
- Albury–Illabo and Illabo–Stockinbingal are in progress;
- Beveridge–Albury has most major sites completed but remaining works continue;
- Narrabri–North Star Phase 1 is completed/operational;
- north-of-Parkes future sections are primarily in **corridor-preservation / future-decision** mode, even where environmental approvals exist.

The distinction matters especially to Toowoomba/Darling Downs analysis. A protected future corridor has option value, but it is not current freight capacity and should not be used as if Brisbane connectivity were already funded.

## Existing-track enhancement can create major capability change

Several Inland Rail sections use existing track and focus on clearance or targeted enhancement for double-stacked freight. Illabo–Stockinbingal is different: it includes about **39 km of new track plus 3 km upgraded track**, bypassing the Bethungra Spiral.

This reinforces a broader VECA transport principle: the useful design space includes targeted new links and branch/bypass works, not only the binary choice between `upgrade everything` and `build HSR/new freight railway`.

## Next high-value extraction: Route Access Standards

ARTC's current Route Access Standard section pages expose the operating data the geometry layer lacks:

- axle-load and speed tables;
- train-length limits;
- loading gauge/outline constraints;
- permanent speeds and special access conditions.

Network Information Books add loops, junctions, sidings and operating diagrams.

The next machine/document extraction should normalize these by corridor section and join them to the existing GA/ARTC geometry. That would enable a real `freight capability` map rather than treating every rail line as equivalent.

## Guardrails

- maintenance/replacement investment is not automatically capacity expansion;
- funded program envelopes are not individually approved projects;
- corridor preservation is not construction commitment;
- double-stack clearance does not by itself establish timetable capacity;
- train-length and axle-load capability must be section-specific;
- freight tonnage, train paths and utilisation remain separate measures from engineering capability.
