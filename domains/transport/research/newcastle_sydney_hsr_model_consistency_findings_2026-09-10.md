# Newcastle–Sydney HSR — model consistency, induced growth and inherited-infrastructure findings

**Research date:** 10 September 2026  
**Domain:** transport  
**Purpose:** test whether HSRA's land-use/housing benefits, transport demand modelling and inherited infrastructure assumptions are internally consistent over the long planning horizon.  
**Status:** curated research synthesis. Evidence, inference and unresolved questions are separated. This note does not authorise a VECA HSR alignment.

## Executive finding

Infrastructure Australia's Stage 3 evaluation identifies a material modelling consistency problem in the Newcastle–Sydney HSR business case:

- transport-demand and land-use models use inconsistent assumptions;
- they use different spatial coverage (Sydney Greater Metropolitan Area for transport demand versus Australia's East Coast for land use);
- they use different demand treatments (including variable versus fixed demand assumptions);
- IA states this has implications for design, network integration, costing, and the representation of benefits and disbenefits.

This is highly relevant because land-use change contributes 58% of monetised benefits, while generalised travel-time savings contribute only 5.8% and Stage 1 results in only about 5% switching from cars.

IA also explicitly says the social-value assessment does not align with the land-use change and that governments need to consider social-infrastructure capacity and the cost of additional services required for the added population.

VECA interpretation: the business case is not yet demonstrated as a fully integrated future-system model in which induced housing, transport access, health/education/service infrastructure, local roads and feeder networks are all consistently resized and costed around the same future settlement scenario.

## 1. Infrastructure Australia's explicit modelling warning

Infrastructure Australia records that assumptions underpinning the transport-demand model and land-use models are inconsistent. IA notes:

- transport-demand modelling covers the Sydney Greater Metropolitan Area;
- land-use modelling covers Australia's East Coast;
- model inputs differ, including variable-demand versus fixed-demand treatments;
- these differences affect model responses to HSR;
- this has implications for design, network integration, costing and the representation of benefits/disbenefits in the economic appraisal.

IA further notes that the post-COVID Transport for NSW travel-zone update (TZP24) was not available for final modelling or sensitivity testing and was not incorporated into the land-use projections.

**Evidence source:** Infrastructure Australia, *High Speed Rail — Newcastle to Sydney, Stage 3 Evaluation Report*, pages 10–11 and note 10.

### VECA significance

This is stronger than a generic 'models could be improved' caveat. It means the two largest analytical systems — where people/households locate and how they travel — were not operating from a single harmonised future-state baseline.

---

## 2. Housing uplift is central to the economic case

Infrastructure Australia reports:

- 46,000 additional households expected because of improved accessibility;
- a further 52,000–104,000 households expected through an assumed 2.8% reduction in average household size;
- IA considers evidence insufficient to support that household-size assumption;
- land-use change contributes 58% of total monetised benefits;
- more productive land use/housing alone contributes 38.7%;
- investment attraction contributes 12%;
- cheaper house prices/rents and associated welfare effects contribute 9.8%;
- generalised travel-time savings for HSR, rail and road users contribute 5.8%.

IA states that excluding land-use and wider-economic benefits reduces the BCR to about 0.2 at a 4% discount rate and 0.1 at 7% in the reported lower-housing/upper-cost case.

### VECA significance

The project is economically presented as a city-shaping and settlement intervention at least as much as a transport-time-saving intervention. Therefore future settlement and service-infrastructure assumptions are decision-critical, not secondary planning details.

---

## 3. Some feeder-network costs are included — but full integration remains unresolved

Infrastructure Australia confirms that the business case does include:

- additional bus fleet costs;
- additional bus network operating costs;
- public-transport feeder networks;
- road connections;
- station precinct/plaza allowances;
- systems integration and operations-control integration.

However, IA also records HSRA's acknowledgement that network/system integration costs require further investigation and that these costs are part of the overall project even where another party may bear them.

### Important distinction

This means it would be incorrect to claim the business case assumes **zero** local transport cost. It does not.

But IA's separate criticism of social infrastructure and inconsistent land-use/transport modelling means the broader future-system cost is still not demonstrated as complete.

---

## 4. Social infrastructure is explicitly incomplete

Infrastructure Australia states that the social-value assessment does not align with the proposal's land-use change, and that governments need to consider:

- social-infrastructure capacity to meet additional needs; and
- costs associated with the additional services.

This directly covers the class of issue raised by hospitals, schools and other services.

### VECA inference

If HSR receives economic credit for inducing additional households into a station corridor, the incremental capital and operating cost of serving those households should be assessed against the same spatial growth scenario. Existing assets can reduce incremental cost, but they should not be treated as indefinitely available spare capacity without capacity/horizon evidence.

## 5. Gosford Hospital illustrates the asset-horizon problem

### Evidence

Gosford Hospital itself dates from 1945, but the relevant modern asset is the major $348 million redevelopment completed around 2019. NSW Health records a new 11-storey building, expanded emergency, ICU, theatres, cardiovascular, maternity, paediatrics, imaging, cancer and additional inpatient capacity.

The Central Coast Local Health District Clinical Services Plan prepared during redevelopment is more revealing for planning horizon:

- the redevelopment's planning horizon was 2026/27;
- it provided additional acute inpatient capacity to meet projected requirements to about 2022, with further capacity needed to support projected growth to 2027;
- the forecasts depended partly on expansion at Wyong Hospital reducing flows to Gosford.

NSW Health later confirmed that the expanded built capacity was designed around demand to 2027.

### VECA interpretation

The hospital is genuine inherited capital and gives Gosford a current service advantage. But its existence is not evidence of sufficient capacity for an HSR-induced 2061 or 2075 population scenario.

A station-selection model that rewards proximity to Gosford Hospital should distinguish:

1. sunk/inherited asset value;
2. current spare/service capacity;
3. expected renewal/augmentation before 2061/2075;
4. incremental capacity required under HSR-induced growth.

## 6. The Central Coast's existing planning system already recognises this problem

The NSW Central Coast Regional Plan 2041 explicitly adopts an **infrastructure-first** approach. Its infrastructure assessment framework is intended to assess:

- capacity of existing infrastructure;
- potential take-up scenarios for future development;
- extent/type/cost/timing of new infrastructure;
- cost effectiveness of supplying infrastructure to homes and businesses.

The plan says those infrastructure costs should influence government decisions about where new homes and jobs are located.

The Central Coast had about 347,500 residents in 2021 and was projected under the 2041 regional plan to reach more than 404,250 by 2041 — roughly 57,000 additional residents requiring about 32,550 additional dwellings.

### Comparison with HSR framing

The HSR business case/public messaging refers to up to about 160,000 additional homes/households across the wider corridor over the longer appraisal period. This is a different order and horizon from the Central Coast's existing 2041 statutory planning baseline.

Therefore the HSR-induced scenario should not simply inherit the 2041 service/infrastructure configuration; it should trigger a new infrastructure-capacity and lifecycle calculation.

---

## 7. Broadmeadow shows the same path-dependency pattern

The Broadmeadow Place Strategy already assumes large-scale redevelopment, government-owned land, new housing and new transport/social infrastructure. Newcastle City Council argued for Broadmeadow as the HSR station partly because at least 17,000 dwellings were already planned and because the precinct had existing/planned transport and government land.

This produces an analytically important distinction:

- **HSR-caused development** versus
- **development already planned in the reference case, accelerated or made more valuable by HSR.**

VECA should not treat all station-precinct housing as additional HSR benefit unless the counterfactual clearly shows that it would not otherwise occur, or would occur in a materially less productive form/location.

---

## 8. Possible circularity / path-dependency mechanism

The evidence supports testing the following mechanism, but does not yet prove that it drove the final scoring:

1. NSW identifies Gosford/Broadmeadow as strategic growth/service centres and invests there.
2. HSRA station MCA rewards existing connectivity, services, government plans, land-use capacity and economic uplift.
3. Those centres therefore score strongly as HSR stations.
4. HSR then enables or accelerates additional housing at those same centres.
5. The economic appraisal credits substantial land-use benefits to that induced/redistributed growth.
6. Additional state/local infrastructure is then required to support the larger population.

The critical unresolved question is whether step 6 is fully deducted in the same economic scenario, and whether baseline housing already planned in step 1 is correctly separated from HSR additionality.

---

## 9. Strong evidence that transport relief is not the dominant quantified outcome

Infrastructure Australia reports:

- only about 5% switching from cars;
- construction emissions of almost 3.7 million tCO2e;
- only about 86,000 tCO2e reduced through removing vehicles from roads;
- approximately 60,000 seats freed on existing rail services by 2061;
- $2.6 billion present-value benefits from reduced crowding, extra passenger/freight capacity and reduced road congestion;
- travel-time savings around 6% of total benefits.

This does not mean HSR has no transport benefit. It means the monetised business case depends much more on induced/redistributed land use than a conventional congestion-relief narrative would imply.

## 10. What must be recovered next

The next evidence targets are now highly specific.

### A. Housing spatial allocation

Recover the geographic allocation of:

- the 46,000 accessibility-induced households;
- the 52,000–104,000 household-size-driven dwellings/households;
- the public '160,000 homes' headline;
- baseline versus HSR scenario by station precinct / LGA / travel zone / SA2 if available.

### B. Additionality and reference case

For Gosford, Broadmeadow, Lake Macquarie and other station areas, identify:

- dwellings already allowed by existing zoning;
- dwellings already in NSW/local growth strategies;
- dwellings already in committed/released development pipelines;
- incremental dwellings attributed only to HSR;
- whether HSR changes timing, density, location or total regional dwelling count.

### C. Infrastructure cost consistency

Determine whether the CBA includes incremental:

- hospital/health capacity;
- schools/TAFE/university capacity;
- water and sewer augmentation;
- electricity/distribution capacity;
- local road upgrades;
- public transport feeder capital and operations;
- open space/community/civic infrastructure;
- emergency services;
- station-area land/property acquisition.

### D. Model coupling

Determine whether the Development Phase is replacing the current split model with a harmonised iterative land-use/transport model, including:

- common geography;
- common base-year and future-year population/employment controls;
- common variable/fixed demand assumptions;
- feedback from induced land use into transport demand;
- feedback from transport congestion/accessibility into land use;
- updated TZP24 projections and subsequent NSW forecasts.

### E. Station MCA

Recover the 26-station longlist and scorecards to determine how much weight was placed on:

- inherited infrastructure;
- future planned infrastructure;
- development capacity;
- travel-time catchment;
- road accessibility;
- public-transport accessibility;
- environmental/engineering cost;
- value capture;
- social infrastructure and future service capacity.

---

## 11. VECA test to build once inputs are recovered

For each station option, distinguish four cost/value layers:

1. **Inherited fixed capital** — usable existing infrastructure at opening.
2. **Committed renewal/growth capital** — would occur without HSR.
3. **HSR-induced incremental capital** — required only because the HSR settlement scenario changes population/land use.
4. **2075 replacement/augmentation cycle** — infrastructure likely to require major renewal regardless of today's presence.

Then compare station options under the **future population they create**, not only today's catchment and infrastructure.

This is consistent with VECA doctrine: existing investment matters, but existing population and infrastructure are not destiny.

## Assurance / limitations

This file is a curated synthesis and does not claim that the complete HSRA business-case model suite has been recovered. Infrastructure Australia's evaluation is the strongest independent source for model limitations but is only an 11-page evaluation summary. The full HSRA business case, modelling reports, land-use outputs, station scorecards and peer-review reports remain critical missing primary evidence. Statements labelled VECA interpretation are hypotheses/inferences to test, not established source facts.
