# Newcastle–Sydney HSR — FOI and business-case appendix audit

**Research date:** 11 September 2026  
**Domain:** transport  
**Purpose:** narrow audit of four unresolved questions: station MCA detail, no-HSR housing baseline, spatial allocation of the 46,000 accessibility-driven households, and treatment of future infrastructure/service costs.  
**Status:** evidence-led reconstruction. Missing public material is distinguished from evidence that something was not modelled.

## Executive finding

This pass strengthens the concern that the economic case and station-selection logic are tightly coupled to land-use assumptions that are not yet publicly transparent enough to reproduce.

Infrastructure Australia confirms that HSRA used a 26-station long list, 13-station shortlist, 18 alignment scenarios and a final three-stage MCA including rapid economic appraisal. However, the published IA evaluation does **not** expose the full 26-station scorecard, the criterion weights, or the individual reasons for eliminating each station.

Infrastructure Australia also confirms that the land-use and transport-demand models use inconsistent assumptions and different spatial coverage. IA states this affects design, network integration, costing and representation of benefits/disbenefits. The same evaluation says the social-value assessment does not align with the land-use change and calls for governments to examine social-infrastructure capacity and the cost of additional services.

The 46,000 additional households are attributed to improved accessibility. Public reporting on the released business case indicates these relocations are concentrated particularly in Newcastle/Hunter, Lake Macquarie and the Central Coast, rather than being distributed uniformly along the corridor. A further 52,000–104,000 households depend on a separate household-size assumption that IA says is insufficiently supported.

The strongest conclusion is therefore not that social infrastructure was entirely omitted, but that **the publicly visible appraisal does not yet demonstrate a fully reconciled future-system cost model in which the settlement uplift, transport demand, and all enabling social/utility infrastructure are evaluated on one consistent spatial scenario**.

## 1. Target 1 — recover the 26-station MCA matrix

### Confirmed

Infrastructure Australia describes the options funnel as follows:

1. 10 strategic alternatives.
2. 26 station locations scored qualitatively against Network and Project objectives.
3. 13 shortlisted station options.
4. 18 alignment scenarios assessed on catchment/accessibility, demand and land use, strategic technical analysis, preliminary rail operations, strategic costings and other qualitative factors.
5. Six shortlisted alignment options.
6. Three-stage MCA including quantitative MCA and rapid economic appraisal.

The preferred strategy was Option 1 (Central) staged toward Option 3 (Western Sydney International).

### Not yet publicly recovered

This pass did not recover a primary public copy of:

- the full 26-station list;
- individual station scores;
- criterion weights;
- normalisation/scoring rules;
- rejected-station reasons;
- full six-option MCA table.

This is a **public-evidence gap**, not proof that the material does not exist.

### FOI path

Infrastructure Australia's FOI disclosure log confirms HSR-related releases:

- 2024-08 — correspondence between HSRA and IA from 1 March to 28 June 2024;
- 2024-14 — correspondence from 20 September to 6 December 2024;
- 2025-02 — re-release of the 2024-14 documents;
- 2025-04 — documents relating to IA's review of the Newcastle–Sydney business case;
- 2025-06 — correspondence/briefing material concerning the evaluation report after July 2025.

The disclosure log notes that some material may be removed after 12 months or supplied only on request because of size/format. The currently indexed public web results did not expose the full HSR attachments directly.

**Next evidence action:** obtain the 2025-04 released bundle from IA's FOI coordinator if no longer downloadable, because IA's published evaluation explicitly refers to methodological issues identified in peer reviews.

## 2. Target 2 — reconstruct the no-HSR housing baseline

### Confirmed

IA says the key land-use benefit driver is **46,000 additional households due to better accessibility**. That wording establishes that these households are intended to be incremental to a reference case.

However, the published IA evaluation does not expose the full geographic no-HSR baseline, including:

- dwelling totals by station precinct/region;
- timing of already-zoned/planned dwellings;
- which NSW TOD or regional-plan dwellings are included in the base case;
- whether HSR changes quantity, timing, location or density of those dwellings;
- how much development is merely redistributed from elsewhere in NSW/Australia.

This matters because Gosford, Tuggerah and Broadmeadow already had significant government-led housing and urban-renewal plans before the HSR business case was complete.

### Additionality risk

The missing question is not simply 'how many homes are built with HSR?'. It is:

> What is the difference between the HSR and no-HSR settlement system after accounting for development already likely to occur under existing planning policy?

Without the reference-case spatial tables, headline housing numbers cannot be independently tested for additionality.

## 3. Target 3 — spatial distribution of the 46,000 accessibility-driven households

### What can now be said

IA confirms the total of 46,000 additional households from improved accessibility.

Public reporting based on the February 2026 released business case says these households are expected to relocate particularly toward **Lake Macquarie and the Central Coast**, with broader reporting also describing the uplift as concentrated across Newcastle/Hunter, Lake Macquarie and the Central Coast.

This is enough to establish that the land-use benefit is geographically selective and therefore directly relevant to station selection.

### What remains unresolved

We still do not have a primary table showing, for each locality:

- base-case households;
- HSR-case households;
- absolute difference;
- percentage uplift;
- year-by-year phasing;
- jobs/employment redistribution;
- assumed dwelling capacity constraints;
- infrastructure constraints or trigger points.

The exact station/precinct allocation therefore remains a high-priority primary-evidence gap.

## 4. Target 4 — which future infrastructure costs are included?

### Confirmed included or partly included

Infrastructure Australia says the cost/economic analysis currently includes:

- systems fit-out and operational integration;
- essential above-ground station operations;
- station precinct/plaza allowances;
- additional bus fleet;
- additional bus network operations;
- proposed public-transport feeder networks;
- road connections;
- over-station development assumptions.

IA also says HSRA acknowledges that integration costs still need further investigation and that such costs remain part of overall project delivery even if borne by different parties.

### Confirmed gap / unresolved treatment

IA separately states:

- the **social-value assessment does not align with the land-use change**;
- governments need further consideration of **social-infrastructure capacity** and **costs associated with additional services**;
- methodological peer-review issues appear not to have been addressed;
- construction disruption impacts were not estimated in the CBA.

The published evaluation does not demonstrate comprehensive inclusion of future incremental costs for:

- hospitals/health-service expansion;
- schools/TAFE/university capacity;
- water supply and treatment augmentation;
- sewer/wastewater capacity;
- electricity distribution augmentation;
- local road capacity beyond direct station connections;
- community/social infrastructure;
- recurrent operating costs of those additional services.

This is not proof every one of these costs is absent from all business-case appendices. It means they are **not shown as fully reconciled in the public evaluation**, and IA explicitly requires further social-infrastructure assessment.

## 5. Transport/land-use model mismatch

IA's criticism is unusually direct. It says the transport-demand and land-use models have:

- inconsistent assumptions;
- different spatial coverage;
- different demand treatments, including variable versus fixed demand.

IA says this affects:

- project design;
- network integration;
- costing;
- representation of benefits;
- representation of disbenefits.

This is central to VECA's concern. If the HSR-case land-use model reallocates tens of thousands of households toward station regions, the transport model should ideally be rerun on the same settlement distribution, with the resulting feeder demand, road demand, conventional rail demand, school/health trips and utility/service consequences feeding back into costs and benefits.

The public material does not yet demonstrate that full closed-loop consistency.

---

## 6. Other important quantitative clues

IA reports:

- approximately 22.7 million HSR trips per annum in 2061;
- only about 5% switching from cars;
- travel-time savings around 6% of total benefits;
- approximately 60,000 seats freed on existing rail by 2061;
- $2.6 billion PV benefit from reduced crowding/additional capacity/reduced road congestion;
- only around 86,000 tCO2e of emissions reductions from vehicles removed from roads versus ~3.7 million tCO2e construction emissions before proposed mitigation.

These numbers reinforce that the Stage 1 case is primarily a **land-use/economic restructuring case**, not a conventional congestion-relief case.

## 7. Preliminary VECA audit conclusion

### Evidence

1. Station selection formally included land-use and accessibility factors.
2. The project's largest monetised benefit is land-use change.
3. The land-use model reallocates households toward particular station regions.
4. The transport-demand and land-use models are not fully consistent.
5. IA says social-infrastructure assessment is not aligned with the land-use change.
6. Some feeder and station-integration costs are included, but further integration costs remain unresolved.

### VECA inference

There is a plausible risk of **path-dependent circularity**:

`existing/planned centre scores well -> station is selected -> accessibility model allocates more households to that centre -> housing uplift becomes a major project benefit -> additional infrastructure required by that uplift is not yet fully integrated into the public appraisal.`

This is a hypothesis to test, not a finding of error.

The test requires the station MCA and the reference-vs-HSR land-use tables.
